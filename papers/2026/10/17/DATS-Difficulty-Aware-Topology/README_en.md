# 2609.13890 — Learning How Much to Collaborate: Difficulty-Aware Topology Selection for Multi-Agent Code Generation (Dats)

## Problem
- Multi-agent code systems deploy with a SINGLE topology fixed at design time — the wrong granularity. The hierarchical role-based mesh (HRM) costs 9.95× the tokens of a single call ($0.94 vs $0.09/problem at Claude Opus 4 prices, $15/M input + $75/M output → effective $36/M).
- But collaboration benefit is NOT uniform: splitting 614 problems (300 APPS intro/interview/competition + 164 HumanEval+ + 150 LiveCodeBench post-cutoff) into difficulty tertiles, the HRM-vs-Single gap is 2.4 points of pass@1 (easy third), 10.7 (middle), 21.1 (hard). On HumanEval+ the five topologies lie within 4.2 points (cheapest at 1/10 the cost); on APPS-Competition the same comparison is 22 points. A fixed-topology system misallocates in both directions: fixed Single forgoes 21 points on the hard tail; fixed HRM spends 10× budget on the easy two-thirds to gain 2–10 points (easy third: $0.94/problem to move 91.7%→94.1%).

## Contribution
- Formulates topology choice as a per-problem constrained selection: max E[s_π(x)] s.t. E[c_π(x)] ≤ B. The Lagrangian relaxation separates across problems, so the optimal policy is pointwise: π_λ(x) = argmax_t [s_t(x) − λc_t]; λ is found by bisection because expected cost is non-increasing in λ.
- Dats (Difficulty-Aware Topology Selector): predict-then-optimize — predicts s_t(x) = Pr[pass | x, τ_t] for all five topologies from the problem statement alone (NO topology is executed at decision time, NO gradients reach the agents), then applies the cost-aware selector with a calibrated λ. This factorisation is worth 6.03 points over direct five-way classification from identical inputs.
- The predictor is a graph network over the TOPOLOGY graph G_T (5 nodes = the 5 topologies, edges along the connectivity order Single–Chain–Star–FullMesh, with HRM linked to Star and FullMesh as the two structures it composes) — NOT a graph over the input statement (no program exists to encode at decision time). Worth 1.7 points over a flat multi-label head (five independent sigmoids), with 13.7K parameters — FEWER than the flat MLP because the connectivity prior is supplied by G_T rather than learned.
- Budget-matched evaluation protocol: a cost-aware router is a one-parameter family of systems, not a system; a shared λ=0.2 spreads realised spend over 21.6 percentage points (30.3%–51.9%) because λ multiplies each method's own score scale — it reverses two comparisons once spend is equalised.

## Method
- Five topologies: Single (1 call); Chain (3 calls: proposer→critic→reviser, sequential criticism); Star (4 calls: hub decomposes into 3 subtasks, 3 workers, deterministic concatenation); FullMesh (3 peers, 2 rounds, 6 calls); HRM (manager above 2 leads, each supervising 2 workers, 5–8 calls). Cost ratios: 1.00/5.10/5.35/8.74/9.95; USD/problem 0.095/0.483/0.507/0.828/0.943; latency 2.6/8.4/26.1/21.7/44.3s. Stable within 3% across backbones (determined by the number and structure of calls, not the model).
- 39 interpretable features in 6 groups: lexical (5: word count, sentence count, average word length, code-span fraction, constraint density — counting "at most"/"exactly"/"for each"); structural (4: worked examples, explicit edge-case discussion, required functions, input–output complexity); complexity (3: nesting hints like "for each pair", large numeric bounds, algorithmic keywords); difficulty (1 scalar: a 5-point rating from a lightweight scorer); semantic (16: leading singular directions of a TF-IDF representation); algorithmic (10: one-hot dominant technique, keyword vote recovering manual labels on 85.5% of problems).
- Predictor: a shared trunk (standardised affine + ReLU) maps 39 features to a difficulty context z; each node is initialised h^0_t = ReLU(W_0[z ∥ e_t] + b_0) with a structural descriptor e_t ∈ R^4 (agent count, edge count, diameter, mean degree of the topology's own communication graph); message passing for L=2 rounds with weights tied across nodes: h^{ℓ+1}_t = ReLU(W_self h^ℓ_t + W_nbr · mean_{u∈N(t)} h^ℓ_u); per-node sigmoid readout ŝ_t = σ(w⊤h^L_t + b). Loss: mean binary cross-entropy over the five node readouts (targets are per-topology outcomes — problems every topology solves teach that cheap suffices; problems none solve teach that the cheapest option is right). Adam at lr 1e-3, early stopping on a 10% internal validation split, 400-epoch cap, training in under 30s on a laptop CPU.
- Selection: ĉ_t = c_t/max c (Single 0.10, HRM 1.0); τ*(x) = argmax_t [ŝ_t(x) − λĉ_t]. At λ=0.06, upgrading Single→HRM must buy at least 5.4 points of predicted success. λ is calibrated by bisection on a 3-fold split of the training fold (target B as a fraction of always-hierarchical cost, 0.5% tolerance or 60 iterations); across the 5 outer folds λ = 0.058, 0.052, 0.065, 0.056, 0.061.
- Budget-matched protocol, 4 steps: (1) declare B as a fraction of the realised spend of a reference policy needing no calibration (always-hierarchical); (2) calibrate EACH method separately by bisection on its own coefficient inside the training folds, never touching the test fold; (3) verify realised spend out of fold with a 0.4pp tolerance (all six methods meet it); (4) compare accuracies only within the matched group; fixed topologies and the oracle are reported separately as frontier locators. Averages 21.4 bisection steps; costs no inference on a cached grid. It does NOT equalise: latency, worst-case cost, or variance.
- Setup: 614 problems; 4 backbones (Claude Opus 4 primary, DeepSeek-V3, GPT-4o-mini, Qwen2.5-Coder-32B); temperature 0.2, 4096-token cap, pass@1 from a single sample, a program is correct only if it passes the complete test suite. Full grid 614×5×4 = 12,280 executions, all cached, ~$1,807 total ($1,753 on Opus 4). 5-fold CV stratified on suite × difficulty tertile, repeated over 5 seeds. Budget B = 40% of always-hierarchical cost = $0.377/problem.
- Baselines: 5 Always-τ policies; Random; Difficulty-Threshold (bins the predicted difficulty rating into topologies, thresholds set on the training fold to meet the budget); Dats-E2E (a five-way softmax on the cheapest successful topology — same features, network, and calibration); MasRouter-lite (logistic model on lexical+structural features); RouterR1-lite (ridge regression on accuracy minus normalised cost); RandomForest-Router (300-tree forest in the same predict-then-optimize scaffold — strongest learned competitor); Oracle.

## Key Findings
- No topology dominates: aggregate ordering Single 62.21% (382/614) < Chain 69.38% (426) < Star 66.61% (409) < FullMesh 70.52% (433) < HRM 73.62% (452); total spread 11.4 points. Chain is AHEAD of Star despite 3 vs 4 calls — decomposition can split a problem along the wrong seam and neither workers nor hub recover; Chain vs FullMesh are indistinguishable (p=0.628) although FullMesh costs 71% more.
- Dats: 77.69% (477/614) — 25 more problems than always-hierarchical at 40% of its budget, 21 more than the strongest learned competitor (RandomForest-Router 74.27%, 456) at the same budget. +4.07 vs Always-HRM (read the other way: 60% of what a hierarchical system spends buys nothing); +6.03 vs Dats-E2E; +9.12 vs Random at two-thirds of Random's cost. Oracle 88.93% (546) at $0.265 — Dats recovers 26.6% of the distance from always-hierarchical to the oracle. Five seeds: 77.52±0.71 (margin ≈ five seed standard deviations).
- Hard tertile: Dats exceeds ALL five fixed topologies (+10.3 points vs HRM); on the easy third Dats tracks HRM (mostly declining to pay for collaboration).
- Budget matching reverses rankings: shared λ=0.2 → RandomForest 76.55% ($0.489, 51.9%) > RouterR1 75.90% ($0.442) > Dats 75.41% ($0.324, 34.4%) — Dats THIRD; calibrated → Dats first at 77.69%, opening a 3.42-point gap over RandomForest. Rank correlation 0.83, yet the first position flips.
- McNemar tests: all eleven comparisons remain significant after Holm–Bonferroni; vs Always-HRM: b=60, c=35, χ²=6.06, p_holm=0.0298.
- Routing distribution: 49.0% of problems → Single, 13.0% → HRM, 38% → Chain/mid. Suite-dependent: 80% of HumanEval+ → Single; 47% of APPS-Competition → HRM. Category gradient: implementation/sorting/string 65–73% Single; graph 49% HRM / 13% Single; dynamic programming 35%/23%; greedy draws the highest Star share of any category (11%) — greedy solutions decompose into a rule and a proof of exchange.
- LiveCodeBench: oracle 88.7% vs Dats 68.7% — the largest oracle gap; contest statements are stylistically uniform, so surface features separate them poorly even though their solutions differ sharply in difficulty.
- Backbones (each retrained, same 40% budget): gain +4.07 (Opus 4), +4.08 (DeepSeek-V3), +4.23 (GPT-4o-mini), +4.07 (Qwen2.5-Coder-32B) — 4.11±0.07, while single-call accuracy spans 62.21→48.37 (13.8 points). Transfer (an Opus-trained router applied unchanged to Qwen2.5-Coder-32B): 61.89%, +1.79 over always-hierarchical but NOT significant (b=59, c=48, p=0.318) — the routing policy transfers, the probability calibration shifts → recalibrate on a few hundred cached problems when switching backbones.
- Cross-domain math (400 problems: 200 GSM8K + 200 MATH levels 1–5, exact match, eight math subject categories replace the algorithmic one-hot, keyword vote 82.3%): Dats 88.0% (352/400) vs HRM 84.5% (338), +3.5 points at 40.3% of its cost ($0.159 vs $0.398/problem), b=25, c=11, χ²=4.69, p=0.030; five seeds 87.84±0.86. The gap widens: GSM8K +2.5, MATH L1–2 +3.0, L3 +9.0, L4–5 +20.9 — the same shape and nearly the same magnitude as code's 2.4/10.7/21.1. 84% of GSM8K answered by a single call vs 21% of MATH; 29% of MATH draw HRM vs 1.5% of GSM8K. Calibrated λ ∈ [0.049, 0.061] overlaps code's [0.052, 0.065] although the two cost tables were measured independently — λ behaves as a price on predicted success, not a fitted constant.
- Calibration: ECE 0.0275, Brier score 0.1723, mild under-confidence in the 0.4–0.7 band where most routing decisions are contested.
- Ablations, feature groups removed: Difficulty −4.24, Algorithmic −3.10, Complexity −2.61, Semantic −2.45, Structural −1.31 (not significant), Lexical −0.82 (not significant) — a deployment could drop eight of 39 features. Architecture: no cost term (λ=0) 83.06% but $0.628 (the unconstrained upper bound of this catalogue); fixed λ=0.2 −2.28 and undershoots the budget by 14%; linear probe −3.75 (a linear model cannot express the difficulty×category interaction); flat multi-label head −1.66; direct five-way classification −6.03.
- Representation ladder (only the encoder changes): Text-GNN (2-layer GCN over the statement graph) −1.14 ns; CodeBERT frozen −0.82 ns; GraphCodeBERT frozen −0.33 ns; GraphCodeBERT fine-tuned end-to-end (125M params, 36 min GPU) +0.49 ns; hybrid of features + frozen GraphCodeBERT +1.30 ns — all p>0.35, spread 2.44pp. 125M parameters of pretrained code knowledge, fine-tuned on the task, buy half a point that cannot be distinguished from noise (seed SD 0.71).
- Router cost: feature extraction under 1ms per problem (linear in statement length); the forward pass is negligible against a single model call (8.4s even in the cheapest topology); training under 30s on a laptop CPU.

## Actionable Insights
- Route topology per problem, not per system: across 614 problems, a fixed HRM spends 10× budget to buy 2.4 points on the easy third but 21.1 points on the hard third — a router calibrated at 40% of HRM cost beats always-HRM by 4.07 points. Example: a coding agent with five pipeline templates (single/chain/star/mesh/HRM) can extract the 39 features (difficulty rating + algorithmic category carry the most signal, −4.24/−3.10 when removed), train the GNN predictor on cached outcomes, bisection λ to the budget, and select the topology before calling any model.
- Comparing cost-aware routers requires printing realised spend beside accuracy and equalising spend: a shared λ=0.2 lets six methods spend 30.3%–51.9% of budget (a 21.6pp spread) and reverses rankings (RandomForest first under shared λ, Dats first once calibrated, by 3.42 points). Example: an agent evaluation dashboard should always print USD/problem + % of reference budget, and calibrate each method by bisection before comparing.
- Hard problems differ by category: graph problems go to HRM 49% of the time, implementation/string 65–73% to Single, greedy to Star (it decomposes into a rule and an exchange proof). Example: as a routing rule of thumb — before running a multi-agent pipeline, classify the dominant technique; only graph/DP/hard problems justify paying 5–10× tokens.
- Predict-then-optimize beats direct classification by 6.03 points: the label "the topology that solved it" is ambiguous when several topologies succeed (38% of problems) and undefined when none do — predict five outcome probabilities and optimize against a cost table (a single scalar λ recalibratable WITHOUT retraining when the budget changes). Example: changing the budget from 40% to 60% only requires re-running bisection on λ, not retraining.

## Assumptions & Limitations
- The catalogue is deliberately small (five hand-designed topologies) — the claim is about the value of CHOOSING among topologies, not the value of any particular topology.
- LiveCodeBench oracle gap of 20 points (88.7% vs 68.7%): stylistically uniform contest statements defeat surface features; the router is "not confused about what topologies do; it is confused about what the problems are".
- Cross-backbone transfer is NOT significant (+1.79, p=0.318): recalibrate λ (and possibly retrain) on a few hundred cached problems when switching backbones.
- The budget-matched protocol equalises expected monetary spend, NOT latency, worst-case cost, or variance — a method that meets the budget by occasionally choosing a very expensive topology is matched to one that never does, and the two may be quite different to operate.
- Math cross-domain: 400 problems, one backbone — establishes the mechanism is not code-specific, but does not characterize routing for mathematics.
- pass@1 from a single sample (temperature 0.2) — a router that only helps under repeated sampling would be solving a different problem (not covered).
- The oracle is unachievable — it bounds the catalogue's potential; 73.4 points of oracle gap remain (88.93−77.69) unclaimed.
