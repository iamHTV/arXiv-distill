# [2609.32827] Improving LLM Collaboration via Multi-Agent Preference Learning

## Core value (1 sentence)
Use preference learning (learning from comparative feedback) instead of fixed scalar rewards to optimize multi-agent LLM collaboration, reaching near MARL-with-oracle-reward performance without needing a perfect metric.

## Problem
Building complete and accurate rewards for multi-agent LLM collaboration is hard in practice — metrics are often unavailable and difficult to aggregate. Prior work assumes scalar rewards are available, leaving preference learning for multi-agent systems underexplored.

## Contribution
- Formulate preference-based multi-agent systems (MAS) from decentralized and centralized collaboration perspectives.
- Introduce MAPL, a general framework for optimizing cooperative MAS from preference feedback, allowing iterative policy updates by comparing current solutions with those generated by comparator agents.
- Instantiate MAPL with MARLHF (multi-agent RLHF with a learned reward model) and MADPO (multi-agent direct preference optimization).
- Experiments on collaborative writing, coding, tool use, and travel planning show MAPL improves collaboration quality and efficiency, approaching MARL with fixed well-defined rewards.

## Method
MAPL initializes by collecting joint histories and corresponding trajectories generated by LLM agents for each task. For each pair of trajectories, an annotator compares their returns, labels the higher-return trajectory as winner and discards ties. Top-k preference pairs by winning return form the replay buffer. Then iterate: either learn a reward model and optimize policies with MARLHF, or directly optimize policies from trajectory preferences via MADPO. Three coordination paradigms: Dec-Dec (decentralized agents + comparator), Cen-Cen (centralized both), Dec-Cen (decentralized agents + centralized comparator).

## KNOWLEDGE — Data & concepts
- CoopHE pass rate: MAPL reaches 65.8%, exceeding strongest single-agent baseline.
- Travel pass rate: MAPL reaches 76.4%.
- MARLHF (Dec-Dec) vs MAGRPO (oracle reward): CoopHE call rate 40.2% vs 37.9%; Travel success 13.1% vs 12.5%.
- MARLHF generally outperforms MADPO on most tasks, but sensitive to data coverage, agent/comparator models, and underlying MARL algorithms.
- Cen-Cen generally performs best because centralized agents exploit more complete information to coordinate.
- Four domains: TLDR (document processing), CoopHE (programming), BFCL (function calling), Travel (travel planning).

## INSIGHT — Mechanism & why
[MAPL works well] BECAUSE the preference dataset has high density of similar-quality samples, providing rich local training signals to distinguish relative quality. [A centralized/larger comparator does not necessarily improve training] BECAUSE of information and realizability gaps between agents and comparators.

## IDEA — Actionable applications
Apply preference learning to multi-agent collaboration: instead of defining hard-to-specify scalar rewards, let users/AI compare two agent output versions and learn from the better one. First step: collect trajectory pairs from two agents working on the same task, label winner/loser, fine-tune a reward model or apply DPO directly.
Apply to business: when multiple LLM agents collaborate on content creation, use preference feedback from end users to coordinate instead of hard-coded metrics.

## WISDOM — General principles

## Assumptions & Limitations
- Assumes an annotator (human or AI) can accurately compare two trajectories and label them.
- MARLHF is sensitive to data coverage, agent/comparator models, MARL algorithm.
- Paper acknowledges: raw LLMs are not inherently cooperative, so initial preference data may contain many non-cooperative trajectories.
- Paper does NOT state: cost of collecting preference feedback at production scale, latency when needing direct annotator.
