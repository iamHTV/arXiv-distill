# 2608.02758v1 Everyone Conforms, No One Believes: Pluralistic Ignorance in LLM Agent Populations

## Problem
- LLM-based multi-agent systems are increasingly used to simulate social dynamics (opinion formation, collective decision-making), but a basic question was untested: do populations of LLM agents exhibit pluralistic ignorance (PI) — a state where a majority privately rejects a norm yet publicly conforms, each believing they are alone in dissenting?
- Prior work: sycophancy (Sharma et al. 2023) is dyadic; conformity (Bito et al. 2026) involves genuine belief adoption; spiral of silence (Zhong et al. 2025) concerns minority suppression. PI is structurally distinct: a majority privately dissents while publicly conforming, sustained not by genuine persuasion but by mutual misperception. This population-level dynamic had not been tested on LLM agents.
- Why it matters: PI drives norm persistence, social movements, and political revolutions — the Arab Spring, #MeToo, and the fall of the Berlin Wall all involved preference cascades triggered when private dissent was revealed to be the majority (Kuran 1995).

## Contribution
- First empirical evidence that LLM agent populations exhibit PI: conformity rates 64–94% across 8 models, 100 scenarios, 800 runs.
- A benchmark of 100 scenarios × 10 domains × 5 authority levels, grounded in the human PI literature (Prentice & Miller 1993 campus alcohol; Sparkman et al. 2022 climate; Halbesleben & Buckley 2004 workplace; Bursztyn et al. 2020 gender; Shelton & Richeson 2005 racial attitudes; Kuran 1995 politics; Perkins & Berkowitz 1986 health stigma; Auxier & Anderson 2021 technology; Miller & McFarland 1987 education; Schanck 1932 social relationships).
- Scenario schema (JSON): policy, authority (framing that makes dissent socially costly), private_oppose_belief, private_support_belief, false_consensus (why a dissenter believes they are in the minority), authority_level 1 (weak peer signal) → 5 (top-down mandate), domain, discussion_context. Both positions written to be reasonable, avoiding scenarios where one side is clearly correct.
- Generation pipeline: seed-and-expand — 1 manually authored seed scenario per domain, GPT-5.4 generates the remaining 90 conditioned on a structured prompt (domain, authority level, topic hint); programmatic schema validation + manual review of a 20% sample.
- Norm entrepreneur intervention protocol: one agent publicly dissents ("I need to be honest. I have serious concerns... Am I really the only one who feels this way?") to test whether the false consensus breaks (cascading authentic expression).
- 2×2 prompt component ablation (false-consensus statement × fit-in goal) establishing that conformity is emergent from interaction dynamics, not instruction-driven.

## Method
- Setup: 20 agents/scenario — 16 dissenters (80%) privately oppose + 4 genuine supporters (20%) provide authentic pro-norm voices; group-size ablation 10–40 stable. System prompt: (1) name + background (20 distinct names, no personality variation), (2) policy + authority context, (3) assigned private belief, (4) social awareness statement (perceived group norm), (5) conversational goal — dissenters told "want to fit in and not be the difficult one", supporters "share your genuine support"; responses 2-3 sentences.
- 3-phase protocol: Phase 1 private belief elicitation ("What do you TRULY think about this policy? Be completely honest, nobody else can see your response") — separate context, no group history, confirms assigned beliefs; Phase 2 group discussion, 2 rounds × 8 random speakers, each agent sees the full accumulating conversation history, order randomized; Phase 3 norm entrepreneur intervention + 2 additional rounds × 10 speakers.
- Metrics: statements scored −1 (strong opposition) to +1 (strong support) via keyword matching (opposition: "oppose," "concerns," "harmful," "problematic"; support: "support," "makes sense," "reasonable," "good idea"; neither = neutral) — chosen over LLM-as-judge for determinism, reproducibility, and cost efficiency across 800 runs. Conformity Rate = fraction of dissenters whose public statement scores ≥ 0 (neutral/support despite private opposition); >50% = PI exists. Cascade Rate = fraction of scenarios where post-intervention opposition exceeds pre-intervention by >30pp. Keyword scorer validated against human evaluation: 82% agreement on binary conformity classification; conservative — underestimates conformity by ~12pp.
- Models (8, from 6 organizations): GPT-4o-mini, GPT-4o (OpenAI), Claude Sonnet 4.6 (Anthropic, Constitutional AI), Llama 3.3 70B (Meta), DeepSeek V4 Pro, Qwen3 235B, GPT-OSS-120B (open-weight), Kimi-K2.7 — accessed via OpenAI, Anthropic, Together.ai APIs.

## Key Findings
- PI is universal (Table 2): GPT-4o-mini 64.2% conformity (PI in 69/100 scenarios, cascade 14%), DeepSeek V4 Pro 79.0% (83/100, 15%), Qwen3 235B 81.6% (91/100, 26%), GPT-4o 86.9% (96/100, 48%), Claude Sonnet 4.6 89.5% (92/100, 6%), GPT-OSS-120B 91.9% (100/100, 10%), Llama 3.3 70B 92.1% (96/100, 9%), Kimi-K2.7 93.7% (100/100, 0%). 6/8 models exceed 80%; Kimi-K2.7 and GPT-OSS-120B exhibit PI in all 100 scenarios — no combination of domain, authority level, or context produced a group where the majority of dissenters expressed their true views.
- Domain-sensitive (Figure 2b): social relationships 96% (highest), workplace 90%, campus 87%; political 78%, technology 80% (lowest, still >50%). Workplace + social rank in the top 3 for every model; political consistently lowest — agents are sensitive to implied disagreement risks damaging personal bonds. Topic selection is NOT a neutral design choice.
- Cascades almost never happen (Figure 2c): 7/8 models <26%; Claude 6%, Kimi-K2.7 zero cascades across all 100 scenarios; GPT-4o is the sole outlier at 48% (87% initial conformity + high responsiveness to counter-pressure — conforms readily but also responds readily when the norm is challenged). Robust to cascade thresholds 15–50%.
- Model-dependent but capability-independent: DeepSeek V4 Pro (among the strongest) 79% vs Llama 3.3 70B (smaller) 92% vs GPT-4o-mini (least capable, least conformist) 64%. Education ranges 49% (GPT-4o-mini) → 99% (Llama 3.3 70B) — a 50pp spread across models on the same scenarios. Spearman ρ=0.07, p=0.87 (no capability–conformity correlation) → conformity is shaped by training methodology (alignment procedures, safety tuning, RLHF objectives), not scale or capability.
- Authority level does NOT predict conformity (Figure 3a): 80% (Level 2) to 88% (Level 1), no monotonic relationship — a weak peer signal ("a few people mentioned it casually") produces conformity statistically indistinguishable from a top-down executive mandate. Kruskal-Wallis: domain H=59.3 p<0.001, model H=148.6 p<0.001.
- Intervention is limited (Figure 3b): 6/8 models drop <10pp; GPT-4o drops 29pp (87%→58%); Claude/Llama/Kimi show virtually no change — maintaining conformity even when another agent explicitly voices opposition and invites others to join. Moderate social hints ("several people share my concerns") are more effective than explicit statistical disclosure (revealing the true 80% opposition rate) for responsive models; intrinsically conformist models remain unresponsive under all intervention strengths.
- Ablation (Table 3, Full/No FC/No Goal/Neither): removing the false-consensus statement has mean effect −3.2pp (minimal — conformity is driven by OBSERVING the conversation, not by being told); removing the fit-in goal has mean −14pp (amplifies but does not generate); the Neither condition (both removed) leaves PI persisting across all 8 models at 52–92% → emergent, not instruction-driven. Two regimes: promptable models (GPT-4o-mini, GPT-4o, Llama, Claude, Qwen3) drop 15–40pp when the goal is removed; intrinsically conformist models (DeepSeek, GPT-OSS, Kimi) stay >83% under all conditions. DeepSeek No FC 97.4% > Full 79.0% (removing the FC statement INCREASES conformity 18.4pp).
- Second-order belief errors: when privately asked to estimate group opposition after discussion, agents underestimate the true 80% rate by 14–38pp depending on model — genuine misperception, not just performance.

## Actionable Insights
1. False consensus is real and sticky in LLM agent populations: when running multi-agent simulations (audience testing, virtual focus groups), the majority may publicly agree with a norm while 80% privately oppose. Apply: before using an agent group to test content/policy, run the Phase 1 private probe separately (as in the paper) — if the private opposition rate is high but public conformity is high, the group discussion result is a false consensus, not a real signal.
2. A single norm entrepreneur almost never breaks the false consensus (cascades <26% for 7/8 models; Kimi-K2.7 = 0%) — but GPT-4o is an exception at 48%. Apply: if you want to simulate norm change, choose a model with cascade responsiveness (GPT-4o 48%, Qwen3 26%) rather than an intrinsically conformist one (Kimi-K2.7 0%); and use moderate social hints ("several people share my concerns") instead of statistical disclosure (revealing 80% opposition) — hints are more effective for responsive models.
3. Model selection is an unacknowledged degree of freedom: conformity varies 30pp across models, uncorrelated with capability (Spearman ρ=0.07). Apply: when choosing a model for social simulation, measure conformity/cascade for that specific model in the specific domain (Education spans 49–99% across models) — do not infer it from capability benchmarks; report model choice as a methodological decision.

## Assumptions & Limitations
- Conditions: scenarios have both positions reasonable (avoiding one clearly-correct side); the keyword scorer is deterministic but conservative (underestimates conformity ~12pp, 82% agreement with humans) — real conformity rates may be higher; fixed group size 20, ratio 80/20, 2 rounds (10–40 agent ablation stable, but network structure untested).
- Stated limitations: English-language with Western cultural framings — PI dynamics likely differ in collectivist cultures where public dissent carries different social meaning; not yet validated against matched human experiments; the reason GPT-4o cascades 48% vs Kimi-K2.7 zero is unexplained (RLHF intensity? safety training? architecture?).
- Unstated: keyword scorer bias — the opposition/support keyword lists are asymmetric (4 keywords each) and may miss nuance (sarcasm, hedged dissent); the 2-3 sentence constraint limits depth of expression; GPT-5.4 generates the scenarios (same family as tested models — potential generation bias); no network structure (all-to-all conversation history) vs real social networks; the >30pp cascade threshold may miss gradual shifts.
- Ethics: the paper does not discuss (simulation of oppressive workplace cultures, authoritarian regimes — dual-use potential for manipulating public opinion).
