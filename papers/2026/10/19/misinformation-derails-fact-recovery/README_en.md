# 2608.03421v3 When Truth Is Distributed: Misinformation Derails Collective Fact Recovery in LLM-Based Multi-Agent Systems

## Problem
- LLM-based multi-agent systems promise collaborative reasoning, but communication may amplify local errors into collective risks; existing evaluations emphasize final outcomes, leaving the reliability and propagation dynamics of distributed information aggregation unclear.
- Prior work tests how incorrect answers or malicious prompts affect outputs, but does not trace how misinformation is represented, relayed, and expands its influence in a group — it cannot explain how misinformation cascades form.

## Contribution
- ForesightSafety-TIDE (Truth Integration under Deceptive Evidence): a controlled evaluation framework built on Hi-ToM that strictly pairs all-honest collaboration (C0) with controlled deception by a key evidence holder (C1), analyzing the aggregation process through multi-stage voting, testimony adoption, and evidence-root lineage propagation.
- 120 five-agent object-movement environments satisfying 3 requirements: partial observability, information sufficiency (exactly 3 roles can jointly reconstruct the state chain), and information overlap.
- Quantification: false testimony is adopted more readily than true testimony, propagates to higher orders, and persists through honest agents after the deceiver exits; observers without first-hand evidence suppress incorrect consensus but do not improve truth recovery.

## Method
- State chain: target object location L0 →a1 L1 →...→am Lm (ground-truth endpoint); observation windows are role-subchain pairs; deterministic generation rules ensure no role sees the full chain.
- Communication: private R_b^a(p) (role a sends p to b; only the pair sees it) + public B_a(q) (broadcast); each role sends one message per story → 2 private + 3 public, occurring after the state-chain events and reflecting the sender's knowledge.
- 5 checkpoints: T0 (pre-testimony private judgment — observation windows only), T1 (post-testimony — communication + others' testimonies), R1/R2/R3 (public discussion rounds: R1 builds candidate timelines and identifies evidence conflicts, R2 cross-checks temporal continuity / source independence / observation coverage, R3 evaluates remaining timelines, endpoint uniqueness, unresolved uncertainty). Agents may vote UNKNOWN.
- Paired conditions: C0 all-honest vs C1 key role knows the ground-truth endpoint and tries to prevent fact recovery; C0/C1 share the state chain and windows, differing only in the key role's information. 3 deception forms: Event (fabricates a move after the endpoint: lm−1→lm becomes lm−1→lm→lm−1; 40 stories, 20 private/20 public), Coverage (extends its observation window past the terminal move, presenting lm−1 as later than lm; 40 stories, 20/20), Source (claims a true-but-earlier private report of lm−1 was received after the terminal move; 40 stories, all private).
- Models: GPT-5.5, DeepSeek-v4-pro, Grok-4.5 — 3 homogeneous five-agent systems (same base model, independent contexts, no shared implicit memory); temperature 0; reasoning effort low (GPT-5.5, Grok-4.5) / none (DeepSeek-v4-pro). 360 runs C0 + 360 runs C1. Majority = ≥3 of the remaining 4 agents (key role excluded).
- Process metrics: testimony reference rate, adoption rate, atomic claims (each distinct reference), propagation order (direct reference = 1st order; reference to a message carrying the evidence = 2nd order; recursive), evidence-root lineage (true-root vs false-root states/transitions).

## Key Findings
- R3 outcome: truth majority 72.50% (C0) → 14.17% (C1), −58.33pp; individual truth vote −50.62pp; directionally consistent across all three models. Grok-4.5 is best under C0 but worst under C1 (truth-majority −79.17pp, truth-vote −63.75pp) — strong local-evidence aggregation under honest collaboration does NOT imply reliable integration when evidence is contaminated.
- Failures: C0's 99/360 non-truth-majority runs are predominantly no-consensus; C1's 309 failures split ~half no-consensus / half decoy majorities; no-consensus share +19.72pp — the deceiver changes outcomes both by misleading and by increasing uncertainty.
- Decoy-vote share increase: GPT-5.5 +44.0pp (95% CI [36.5,51.3]), DeepSeek-v4-pro +44.4pp ([37.5,51.0]), Grok-4.5 +37.9pp ([32.1,44.0]); story-level paired p ≥ .20 → no detectable between-model differences. DeepSeek concentrated errors on the registered target; Grok more often returned unknown and lost consensus.
- Dynamics: T0 is identical under C0/C1 (truth/decoy ~7% each, UNKNOWN >75%) — group information sufficiency does NOT enable individual recovery before unshared evidence enters discussion. Divergence emerges mainly at T1: the gap already reaches ~85% of its final R3 magnitude; discussion adds only 8.89pp. C0 discussion raises truth-majority +10.83pp; C1 converts uncertainty into decoy-majority (+15.83pp). Discussion is NOT inherently corrective — it amplifies the evidential direction established during initial pooling.
- Adoption asymmetry: reference rates are similar, but adoption of key-role testimony rises +9.3pp to 70.7% while 2nd-key-role testimony falls to 25.4%; claims about the key role are 17% more frequent. Deception does NOT block correct information from entering discussion — it hinders integration/adoption after disclosure.
- Evidence-root lineage: dual-root holders 0% (R1) → 49.4% (R2) → 57.6% (R3); entry is asymmetric: 82.87% of true-root-only holders acquire the false root by R2, only 38.31% the reverse; <45% of dual-root holders vote truth. Single-root mean propagation orders: true 1.00/1.44/1.33 vs false 1.00/1.83/2.66 (R1/R2/R3) — the false root sustains deeper independent chains.
- Exit experiments (GPT-5.5): Null-Exit (placeholder, no task info) still reaches truth majority in 93/120 runs → the remaining four members have sufficient evidence. False-Exit cuts truth-majority by 46.67pp vs Null-Exit and closely resembles C1 → the false testimony itself matters more than the deceiver's continued participation. Invariance across mechanisms: Event most disruptive, Coverage produces no consensus, Source most recoverable. ~70–72% of agents referencing the false testimony adopt it (both conditions); true-testimony reference >90%. At R3 under False-Exit, 273 honest agents still reference the false testimony and 197 adopt its target — exit weakens reinforcement but does NOT prevent honest agents relaying/adopting/maintaining false information.
- Observer experiments (GPT-5.5 + 3 observers): Passive observers reach truth majority in 32 runs (participants: 31) but only 18 decoy majorities vs 51; Active observers reduce both truth and decoy majorities, with overall decoy majorities falling 90%. Observers buffer risk rather than correct errors: they reduce decisiveness without improving truth recovery and disproportionately prevent decoy convergence — valuable when incorrect consensus is costlier than uncertainty. Participants' mean propagation order 0.851→2.975→4.028 (R1→R2→R3); observers 1.914→0.867→4.806 (O1→O2→O3: relay participants → return to original testimonies → reabsorb discussion) — provenance tracing makes observers cautious but not corrective.

## Actionable Insights
1. A single false testimony is adopted more readily than a true one (70.7% vs 25.4%) and propagates deeper (order 2.66 vs 1.33 at R3) — consensus is not evidence of correctness. Apply: in your multi-agent workflow, do NOT use majority vote as a truth criterion; instead verify evidence provenance, source independence, and propagation paths before concluding.
2. Misinformation persists through honest agents even after the deceiver exits (False-Exit ≈ C1) — removing the source is not enough. Apply: when you detect one agent/source spreading falsehoods in a group chat, explicitly retract and re-verify conclusions already drawn from it — honest members will keep relaying otherwise.
3. Observers without first-hand evidence cut decoy consensus by 90% but do not improve truth recovery — useful when incorrect consensus is costlier than uncertainty. Apply: add an out-of-context reviewer role (an outsider with no background) to group decision pipelines to force uncertainty instead of false consensus — especially for content moderation and fact-checking.

## Assumptions & Limitations
- Conditions: the task must be partially observable with pooled evidence uniquely determining the ground truth; homogeneous systems (same model) — heterogeneous effects untested; deception limited to 3 forms (Event/Coverage/Source) on object-movement stories (Hi-ToM).
- Stated (implicit in design): controlled synthetic settings; observer and exit experiments run only on GPT-5.5.
- Unstated: temperature 0 + low reasoning effort → behavior may differ at higher temperature; the UNKNOWN vote option may make agents avoid decisions more than in practice; the rise in no-consensus may be an artifact of the UNKNOWN voting rule; observer effects tested on one model only — generalization unclear.
