# [2609.18283] Where Should Agents Live? — agentic-eCAL: Energy-Memory Characterization of Agentic AI for the Edge-Cloud Continuum

## Problem
- Prior AI lifecycle metrics (original eCAL, single-model serving studies) evaluate isolated single-model inferences or overlook multi-agent execution graphs entirely → operators lack a foundational model to decide whether distributed agent communication incurs meaningful energy cost and where agent teams should physically reside across edge-cloud tiers. Three properties make accounting differ from single inference: (a) off-the-shelf models → embodied pre-training energy is shared; (b) the unit of work is a workflow, not an inference — accumulating transcripts make token volume (and energy) super-linear in steps; (c) heterogeneous components (tool execution E_tool, vector retrieval E_ret, 7-layer OSI transmission E_tx).

## Contribution
- agentic-eCAL: generalizes eCAL (Energy Cost of AI Lifecycle) to directed multi-agent workflows as a per-bit metric [J/b]: (E_W + γe·(E_emb + E_emb,ret)) / B_useful; reduces to eCAL's single-inference metric when K=1, co-located, no tools/retrieval.
- Two-rate closed-form single-call energy model: E_call ≈ c_pre·p_in + c_dec(b)·p_out — compute-bound prefill (batch-independent) + memory-bandwidth-bound decode (weights amortized over batch b, KV-cache floor c_0).
- NVML-metered empirical validation on A100/H100 with vLLM: 270 configurations per model (Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B), 2–30 agents, batch b ∈ [2,256]; R² = 0.996, MAPE ≈ 10–12%.

## Method (core)
- Workflow energy: E_W = (1+γ_v)·[ΣE_call + ΣE_tool + ΣE_ret + E_tx], γ_v ≈ 10% virtualization/orchestration overhead; per-site memory feasibility: β·N_params + γ·Σc_a ≤ M_u (β = 2 bytes/param, γ = KV bytes/token).
- c_dec(b) = a_dec/b + c_0; fitted coefficients: Qwen2.5-7B a_dec=3.2, c_0=0.02 J/token; Llama-3.1-8B 3.3, 0.06; c_pre ≈ 0.02–0.03 J/token (η_pre 0.74–0.85). Crossover: a call becomes prefill-dominated when p_in/p_out > c_dec(b)/c_pre → at production batch, workflow energy scales as K^a with 1≤a≤2 (small batch decode-bound a→1; large batch prefill-bound a→2).
- History-carrying loop: p_in^(k) ≈ p_sys + s·k → Σp_in = sK²/2 quadratic, Σp_out = sK linear; automatic prefix caching (APC) cuts co-located redundant prefill to O(K), while distributed microservices pay full transcript re-prefill.
- Retrieval: E_ret = β·N_emb·p_q·p_emb/(η_emb·Π_emb) + α_idx·d_emb·log(M_vec); growing context 2K→10K tokens raises energy/token ~3× (70B models).
- OSI 7-layer transmission: protocol overhead 100–200% (L2 MAC…L7 gRPC/HTTP-2), HARQ retransmission R≈1.5–2.0 at 5G cell edge; bearer intensities: 5G RAN ε≈10⁻⁶, metro/fixed 10⁻⁷, optical backbone 10⁻⁸ J/b.
- 8 orchestration topologies (Star, Persona-Star, Proposer-Critic, Tournament, Tree, Chain, Cascading-Chain, Diamond): summed prefill = N·p_0 + E·τ — only edge count E matters, graph shape does not; six architectures have E≈N−1, Diamond E≈1.5N, Cascading-Chain E≈5N.
- Hand-off protocols (K=3, 4-agent workflow): full transcript 0.33 Mbit/session, increment 0.051 (6.5× less), bounded summary 0.079, KV-cache migration 18.9 Gbit (cache entry 131 kB/token vs 17 bits per token) → dynamic range 3.7×10⁵ (protocol) vs 4.8× (architecture) vs ~10³ (bearer). Parity bearer intensity ε* for text > 3×10⁻³ J/b (no deployed bearer reaches it); KV migration ε* falls to 5.1×10⁻⁸ J/b by K=6 → attention state effectively non-transportable, binding context to its local accelerator.

## Key Findings
- Inter-agent text transport < 0.25% of workflow energy (E_tx/E_prefill ≈ 1/496 for a 2,700-token hand-off over 5G); 1,290 boundary-crossing tokens cost 8.9 mJ (metro fibre) to 0.52 J (loaded edge cell) = 0.003–0.186% of E_W at b=64. The dominant energy cost of distribution is the additional inference and context processing induced by communication, NOT the text transmission itself.
- History carry at K=6: +32.8% energy on Qwen2.5-7B (89 J vs 67 J), +21.7% on Llama-3.1-8B (185 vs 152 J); Llama uses ~2× Qwen's energy (c_0 0.06 vs 0.02 J/token; γ 131 vs 56 kB/token).
- Batching saves 38.1% at N=2 (130 vs 210 J) but only 15.4% at N=30 (4,400 vs 5,200 J) — batch amortization cannot overcome the quadratic prefill explosion of large dense teams; energy-vs-N exponent a rises 1.10→1.42 (Qwen) and 1.02→1.17 (Llama) as b goes 2→256.
- Placement is governed by memory: ≥64 GB hosts all four weight classes (incl. 4-bit 70B); concurrent 10-agent sessions range 1–2 (8 GB Orin) to 59 (A100); within the 8–16 GB class capacity varies 16× — GPT-OSS-20B (γ=48 kB/token) sustains 112 sessions, Qwen2.5-7B (56) 87, OLMo-2-7B (512) 7 → the binding constraint on serving density is KV width γ, not model weights.
- 4-agent RAG case study (planner→retriever→analyst→writer, Llama-3 8B): 4,990 prompt vs 1,020 generated tokens (ratio 4.9); transcript supplies 56% of the writer's prompt; retriever is least efficient (0.034 J/bit vs 0.0116–0.0123 at b=64, 2.8×, prefilling 2,000 tokens to emit 120); workflow 0.0162 J/bit vs calls alone 0.0146 (tool + retrieval + 10% orchestration).
- ETSI ZSM infra-repair benchmark (ephemeral k3s edge clusters, 24 incidents, 60 min/50 topology turns, ≥3 runs/config): Qwen3.5-9B topologies consume 257.0–1029.2 kJ/run (4.0×) scoring 50.0–64.6%; Single ReAct solves 61.7%, only P+R+W+V beats it (64.6%) at 2.9× energy; kJ per successful repair: Single 17.4 vs multi-agent 29.0–79.2. Qwen2.5-7B: Single 9.2%, multi-agent topologies score 0.4–4.6% (WORSE) despite ≤2611.1 kJ/run; kJ/success 49.7 vs 320.0–5222.3 (up to 23.9× energy, 4.0× for 9B). PS+V: score 45.8→59.7% for 51.5→68.4 kJ; PS cost rises 31.2→79.2 kJ/solved task from 1→5 proposers while score stays ~constant.
- Amortization: Llama-3 8B embodied 0.91 GWh (1.3M H100-hours) dominates per-invocation agentic-eCAL until G ≈ 1.2×10¹⁰ invocations (20–60× the BLOOMz inference-parity threshold of 2–6×10⁸); Llama-3 70B reaches operational parity at 0.66× the 8B's invocations (pre-training energy grew only 4.9× from 8B→70B while operational cost scales with parameters).

## Actionable Insights
- Decide placement by VRAM fit (weights + per-session KV cache), not bandwidth: text hand-offs cost <0.25% of workflow energy — stop optimizing network location; optimize memory density. Example: pick the host of your agent team by γ (KV kB/token) × concurrency budget — GPT-OSS-20B hosts 112 sessions vs OLMo-2-7B's 7 on the same accelerator.
- Bound context growth in multi-step pipelines: transcript carry is quadratic in K (+32.8% at K=6); hand off increments (0.051 Mbit) or bounded summaries (0.079) instead of full transcripts (0.33 Mbit, 30× growth from K=1→6). Example: each agent step sends only a diff + 200-token summary rather than full history.
- Keep teams small and sparsely coupled: all-to-all implies Θ(N²) prefill; batching saves only 15.4% at N=30; independent agents collapse to linear scaling. Example: replace a 30-agent debate (32–158× a single call) with a sequential chain (E≈N−1 edges) — same information flow at far lower energy.
- Multi-agent is NOT automatically better: benchmark kJ per solved task before adopting — a single ReAct agent beat most multi-agent topologies (61.7% vs best-multi 64.6% at 2.9× energy; on 7B the single agent was BETTER: 9.2% vs 0.4–4.6%). Example: measure a one-agent baseline's cost-per-solved-task before buying an orchestration stack.
- Retrieval is a hidden cost center: the retriever costs 2.8× per useful bit (prefills 2,000 tokens to emit 120); +10K context tokens ≈ 3× energy/token on 70B. Example: cap retrieved chunks and reuse prefix caching (TTFT −4×; tuned RAG pipelines save up to 60%).

## Assumptions & Limitations
- Coefficients must be calibrated on real GPUs (NVML metering); datasheet HBM bandwidth gives a lower bound for c_dec (serving stacks sustain ~70% of peak) → magnitude error ~1/3 while the form holds.
- Paper-stated: prefill assumes the full prefix (conservative upper bound — APC/RadixAttention cuts TTFT up to 4×); MoE models must use active routed parameters N_act, not N_params; calibration captures A100 PagedAttention fragmentation — newer FP8/FP4 architectures will shift the decode roofline; embodied-energy amortization volume G is hard to track across redistributed open-weight models.
- [MY OWN] Benchmark domain is narrow (24 Kubernetes infra-repair incidents) — transfer to content/marketing workflows is unmeasured; case-study token budgets are stipulated, not measured; tool/embedding energies are analytical, not directly metered.
