# 2610.19051v1 Smart Alone, Blind Together: Why Accuracy Fails to Certify Theory of Mind in Multi-Agent Teams

## Problem
- Coordinators that act on what they infer about teammates (theory of mind — ToM) are usually certified by the accuracy of those inferences. Accuracy is the wrong certificate for two reasons. (1) Cost: the break-even accuracy depends on what a wrong action costs, so the bar belongs to the deployment, not the module. (2) Dependence: a module's errors are rarely independent, and dependence has a sign — positively associated errors help where their damage overlaps and hurt where it compounds, so an accuracy threshold fails in a predictable direction; once wrong beliefs interact and acting on them can do harm, no accuracy-based rule is right for every module. Prior work evaluates ToM modules mostly by inference accuracy and deploys when accuracy clears a threshold; a wrong inference does not merely fail to help — it moves the system somewhere a passive, belief-free policy would not have gone, so below some accuracy, acting on ToM is worse than not acting at all.

## Contribution
- A cost-based account of when acting on ToM pays, with preregistered tests in three domains (task assignment, Level-Based Foraging, Hanabi); 73 preregistered predictions (48 held, 3 held in part, 21 failed, 1 unscored).
- A theory of certification under correlated errors: Theorem 1 (sign condition — supermodular benefit means associated errors help, submodular means they hurt), Corollary 1 (the direction in which accuracy fails), Theorem 2 (a certificate seeing only order-k error rates certifies every module iff no interaction of more than k beliefs is non-zero — accuracy (k=1) certifies only when no wrong beliefs interact).
- Evidence from trained classifiers, a Bayesian module, five language models (four Claude, one GPT) and three human datasets: accuracy fails in BOTH directions (approving harmful deployments in Hanabi, rejecting beneficial ones in task assignment) and buys far less redundancy than independence predicts.
- The Blind-Spot Gate: a certificate that prices error costs, pairwise interactions, and measured dependence WITHOUT deploying the module — as safe as an A/B test with a 95% bound on held-out data, better than direct measurement when games of the module are scarce, and lower in regret than accuracy on out-of-sample modules (preregistered).

## Method
- Normalized benefit B = (score − do-nothing)/(informed − do-nothing); B(a,h) = a + (1−a)ℓ(h); break-even accuracy a*(h) = |ℓ(h)|/(1+|ℓ(h)|) when ℓ(h)<0.
- Proposition 1: the threshold is non-decreasing in harm h; errors that cannot reach the costly channel (the events through which a wrong action does harm) have an h-independent threshold.
- Proposition 2: a unit lost when ANY belief is wrong (concave φ, series system) → damage overlaps (supermodular); a unit lost only when ALL are wrong (convex φ, parallel/redundancy) → damage compounds (submodular).
- Proposition 3 (redundancy among observers): n observers sharing a target-level error probability p_t have Pr[all wrong] = E[p_t^n] ≥ (E p_t)^n; n_eff = ln Pr[all wrong]/ln E p_t ∈ [1, n]; a mixed team fails together at most as often as the geometric mean of its members' clone teams.
- Blind-Spot Gate: B̂ = b0 + Σ_u q_u c_u + Σ_{u<v} (q_u q_v + λ Δ_uv) I_uv, act iff B̂ > 0 — c_u = cost of belief u alone wrong, I_uv = pairwise interaction (from paired single- and pair-injection replays via ThresholdProbe), q_u = the module's error rate on belief u, Δ_uv = Pr[both wrong] − q_u q_v (measured dependence). At λ=1 with the module's own costs and interactions this is the order-2 certificate of Theorem 2. It never deploys the module to certify.
- Domains: task assignment (12 workers, 10 hidden rules — global, local, hermit, cascading; 400-round episodes; harm h ∈ [0,1] scales a wrong assignment from a wasted round to ejecting the worker); LBF (Jumanji 10×10 grid, 6 agents, 8 foods, levels 1–3); Hanabi (4 players, a hidden hint convention per player — dialect A vs B; W=12 directed beliefs; the belief-free team scores 11.1–13.2 of 25, the informed team 18.3–20.0; harm is life tokens).
- Modules: classifiers (accuracy 0.42–0.53), an LBF level-inference module, a Hanabi Bayesian module that infers dialects publicly so every observer shares its errors, and LLMs — Claude Haiku 4.5, Sonnet 4.5, Sonnet 5.5, Opus 5.5, GPT-5.4-mini (250 fresh games, 4 samples per model per game at temperature 1; clone team / public team / mixed team). Protocol: preregistered, 2,000 episodes per cell (10,000 for the one-life test), surrogates with independent errors, 95% cluster bootstraps.

## Key Findings
- Thresholds move with cost: task assignment 0.655 (h=0) → 0.779 (h=1); LBF 0.32 → 0.64 (penalty 0→1); Hanabi 0.621 (five lives) → 0.845 (one life). Eq (1) with one free parameter per harm explains ≥99.8% of variance above accuracy 0.5. Channel: under-estimated levels have threshold 0.697 at every harm (they never reach the penalty); same-type rule swaps 0.261 (five curves identical); cross-type errors 0.693 → 0.819. The Shapley value assigns 91% of the knee's depth to self-organization. ThresholdProbe predicts the threshold within 0.03 (task assignment), 0.05 (30/31 LBF cases), 0.01 (Hanabi, two to five lives).
- Task assignment (costly errors rare): classifier accuracy 0.42–0.53 is below the independent-error threshold at every harm, so an accuracy rule never deploys them — yet acting on their beliefs beats doing nothing in 33 of 35 (module, harm) cells (up to +0.76, where independent errors score −0.40 to −1.00). Coherent errors: same-class workers share the same wrong label in 49–54% of pairs vs 33–46% for confusion-matrix draws; an independent surrogate under-predicts benefit by 0.08–0.19; under 0.4% of errors violate a task type.
- Hanabi (damage compounds): beliefs shared across observers cost 0.17–0.35 of the achievable benefit with two or more lives (16/16 cells), raise the threshold by 0.04–0.11, and lose 28–36% more critical cards. The Bayesian module behaves like the shared-error surrogate (within 0.05 in 27/30 cells), loses 0.07–0.31 to independent errors, and accuracy approves two deployments that lose 0.23 to the belief-free team.
- LLMs: all-three-observers-wrong happens 4.7–18.6× the independent rate for Claude models (preregistered, held) and 3.5× for GPT-5.4-mini; an item-response null (teammate difficulty + model accuracy) already predicts joint errors 3.8–11.2× the independent rate; the residual ×model grows with accuracy (1.66 for Opus 5.5). Redundancy: per-belief error falls from 32% (GPT-5.4-mini) to 11% (Opus 5.5), but all-three-wrong falls only from 16% to 9% (independence predicts 3.4% → 0.15%); three observers are worth 1.64 down to 1.11 independent ones; when one sample misreads a hinted teammate, the others do too 41% (GPT-5.4-mini) and 78% (Opus 5.5) of the time. Four mixed Claude models misread jointly 7.5% vs 9.0% for Opus clones.
- Both directions: Hanabi with ≥2 lives — every Claude clone team does worse than independent errors (12/12 cells, −0.01 to −0.26) and accuracy approves five harmful deployments. Task assignment — every team is below threshold at every harm, yet Sonnet 5.5 beats not acting at all three harms and Haiku and the mixed team at h=0 → five false rejections, zero false approvals.
- Robustness: relabelled conventions (5,000 fresh calls): copies worth only 1.08–1.51 observers, and accuracy approves four more harmful deployments; maximum reasoning effort (Opus 5.5, 13× tokens): no more accurate on hinted teammates (0.869 at both efforts), copies worth 1.10 vs 1.11 observers.
- Humans: CIFAR-10H (2,571 people, 10,000 images), CREMA-D (2,442 people, 7,442 clips), Good Judgment Project (6,608 forecasters, 497 questions): three readers are all wrong 39×, 2.7×, and 2.7× the independent rate; the most accurate tenth of forecasters fails as a redundant trio on 2.95% of questions (independence predicts 0.15%); forecasters on the same team are wrong together 1.15× (pairs) and 1.21× (triples) as often as forecasters from different teams of the same year (permutation p<0.004); forecasters sharing only randomly assigned training share NO excess errors (pairs 1.00) — sharing an information channel, not a method, creates shared errors.
- Blind-Spot Gate (57 language-model cells, 20 random splits): the accuracy rule has mean regret 0.056, 3.2 false approvals, and a significant false approval in 20/20 splits; the gate (rule E, λ=1) has regret 0.021, 0.7 false approvals, and significant false approvals in 0/20 splits — as safe as the safety test (an A/B test with a one-sided 95% bound: regret 0.021, 0 significant false approvals) without deploying the module. Out-of-sample (preregistered): on seven task-assignment classifiers, accuracy (0.43–0.54) rejects every cell while the gate approves 19/21 on average (all 18 cells of the six larger classifiers in every split, all significantly beneficial), defers the one harmful cell in every split, makes no significant false approval, and matches direct measurement (regret 0.004 vs accuracy's 0.479); on the Bayesian module, accuracy makes a significant false approval in 17/20 splits (including both harmful deployments) while the gate never approves them (regret 0.009 vs 0.029, lower in 18/20 splits). With few games: the gate beats direct measurement up to 20 games (0.034 vs 0.053 at 10 games, in at least 17/20 splits), levels at 40, and direct measurement leads from 80. Error load above four wrong beliefs per episode → hand to direct measurement (regret 0.021 → 0.014).
- Deferral: a Bayes act-or-defer rule that withholds convention-dependent hints from teammates the module has not yet read is beneficial in all 30 fresh-seed cells (minimum +0.30) and beats acting on every belief by at least +0.09, including after a single round of watching (+0.30 vs −0.18 to −0.74).

## Actionable Insights
- Certify the deployment, not the module: before letting an agent act on inferred teammate states, measure (a) the cost of one wrong action by paired injection, (b) the sign of error interactions from the task's structure (series vs parallel), (c) how often errors co-occur. Example: a panel of three LLMs reviewing contracts — measure how often all three miss the same defect, not their individual accuracy.
- Copies of one model are worth nearly ONE observer: three Opus 5.5 copies are worth 1.11 independent observers (all-wrong 9% vs 0.15% predicted); mixing models cuts joint failure (7.5% vs 9.0%). Example: ensemble voting with the same model and prompt buys almost no redundancy — diversify model families or prompts instead.
- Defer on unread targets: the act-or-defer rule is beneficial in 30/30 cells (minimum +0.30) — when the ToM module has no evidence about a target, fall back to a safe passive policy rather than act. Example: an agent that cannot read a teammate's convention should use only hints safe in every dialect.

## Assumptions & Limitations
- Conditions: Theorem 1 needs positively associated errors and a supermodular or submodular benefit (Proposition 2 gives sufficient conditions); Theorem 2 needs all-beliefs-wrong to hurt and all-right to help; the gate is an order-2 certificate — exact when no interaction of three or more beliefs is non-zero; the gate needs a simulator that accepts injected beliefs; its tables assume a module's errors are unrelated to the episode state that sets their cost.
- Limitations acknowledged: the domains are controlled benchmarks with known hidden variables (which is what enables injecting errors of known kinds); the human data show the same knee and the same two directions; the language-model tests use one prompt family (relabelled once); the human team analyses combine judgments made independently; the gate is a second-order certificate — modules making many coherent errors at once need direct measurement (error load above four).
- Not stated: the Hanabi Bayesian module's "public" inference is a specific construction — production LLM ToM does not necessarily share inferences publicly; n_eff is measured for three observers only (n>4 untested); the task-assignment harm h is a stylized scaling, not a real ejection cost; the human datasets (CIFAR-10H, CREMA-D, GJP) are perceptual/forecasting, not live multi-agent coordination; preregistration files and code are available on request only.
