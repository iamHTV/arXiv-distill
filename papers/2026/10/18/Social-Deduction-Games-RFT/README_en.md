# 2610.04261v1 Playing social deduction games with reinforcement fine-tuned large language models

## Problem
- Reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) is increasingly used in applications where LLMs interact with humans and other agents, yet it is unclear how RFT shapes the social reasoning of LLM agents. Prior work mostly EVALUATES the model+prompt as a static object; social competence is better understood as a set of behaviourally grounded functions. The core difficulty: terminal win-loss outcomes are a sparse and noisy signal for socially interactive learning — a win or loss is the final consequence of a long causal chain (hidden-role inference, public communication, voting, role-specific actions, other agents' mistakes, stochastic events); the same outcome can follow from very different behaviours, and an individually useful action can occur in a losing trajectory. Outcome-only RFT compresses these causes into a single sparse signal, leaving a difficult multi-agent credit-assignment problem.

## Contribution
- AgentEvolver Game Arena: a multi-agent environment with hidden roles, private observations, public discussion, and consequential group decisions; a turn-based prompt-chaining protocol; trajectories τ = {(z_i, x_{i,t}, y_{i,t}, a_{i,t}, s_t)}; a controlled-policy setting (a subset C is trainable, the rest are fixed opponents) plus a same-side variant (the trainable policy controls ALL roles of one side with shared parameters and role-conditioned updates — one game yields multiple training trajectories).
- Decomposition of social competence into two measurable components: social reading (inferring latent social state from dialogue and partial observations — the reward compares the belief state against the hidden state: F1 for set-valued predictions, exact-match for categorical) and social influence (changing other players' beliefs and decisions through communication — the reward measures the intended social target against others' subsequent actions). Three macro-families of games: Elimination (Werewolf/Mafia, One Night Werewolf), Institution (Avalon, Secret Hitler), Asymmetry (Spyfall, Deception: Murder in Hong Kong).
- A reward decomposition for joint multi-agent social-cognitive RFT: R(τ) = λ_read·R_read + λ_inf·R_inf + λ_aux·R_aux − λ_fmt·P_fmt − λ_leak·P_leak (P_fmt penalizes invalid structured outputs; P_leak penalizes illegal public disclosure of hidden information); the terminal win rate is logged but NOT used as the primary online reward — it serves as a held-out diagnostic. Optimization uses GRPO (group-relative advantage, clipped policy-gradient with a KL penalty, no critic).
- Evidence that RFT changes social behaviour in a reward-dependent way, with a blinded human evaluation (178 ratings on a 5-point scale) and cross-model generalization (9 opponents, 100 games each).

## Method
- Models: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, Qwen3-4B-Instruct, GLM-4-9B-Chat, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, DeepSeek-7B (replication). Mixed-role setup: the focal player is sampled across roles (innocent, adversarial, special-information) to prevent overfitting to a fixed perspective.
- Cross-game outcome-only RFT: the reward is 1 if the focal side wins, 0 otherwise.
- Social reading experiments: (1) hidden-state inference — Avalon predicts team alignment, Werewolf/Mafia predicts the wolf set from a partial transcript; (2) dynamic belief updating — Avalon grouped by mission, Werewolf focal villager's belief by successive days; (3) role-specialized — the Avalon Assassin fixed-role variant with an outcome-based reward, compared against Merlin, Servant, and Minion.
- Social influence experiments: (1) vote steering — the One Night Werewolf Minion in a self-selection variant (the Minion chooses its own target and is rewarded if other players' final votes concentrate on that target while avoiding the Werewolf); (2) team approval — Avalon: the focal agent produces one public response before the approval vote; other agents vote at baseline (without the response) and then vote again (with it); the reward is the post-response outcome matching the target direction plus the change in other players' votes.
- Multi-agent social-cognitive RFT: prompt-level ablations (no scaffold / SR only / SI only / SR+SI; Werewolf good side; win rate metric; fixed conditions) plus full-game RFT (six-player Werewolf, nine-player Werewolf, Avalon) with online rewards emphasizing intermediate social-cognitive signals; cross-model evaluation (the learned nine-player good-side policy vs 9 wolf-side opponents including Qwen3.7-Max, Qwen-Plus, Qwen-Max, Kimi-K2.5, 100 valid games per opponent); human evaluation (blinded complete nine-player games, 74 matched base-RFT pairs, ratings 1–5 on five dimensions: overall value, social-reading quality, social-influence & coordination, human-like gameplay, strategic correctness; evaluators explicitly instructed NOT to high-score merely because the good side won).

## Key Findings
- Outcome-only RFT is unstable: Secret Hitler strongest (final ~0.63, average ~0.61); Avalon hard (0.23→0.39, average 0.25); Werewolf/Mafia peaks at 0.88 but ends at 0.38; One Night Werewolf averages 0.52, non-monotonic; Spyfall flat (average 0.40, final 0.50); Deception peaks at 0.75, final 0.50, average 0.48. → directly optimizing terminal win-loss does NOT reliably produce general social-deduction ability.
- Social reading (hidden-state inference) improves clearly across models: Avalon Qwen2.5-7B 0.55→0.78, Qwen2.5-14B 0.65→0.71, Qwen3-4B 0.62→0.70, GLM-4-9B 0.58→0.69; Werewolf/Mafia Qwen2.5-7B 0.46→0.65, Qwen2.5-14B 0.47→0.65, Qwen3-4B 0.33→0.44, GLM-4-9B 0.29→0.50, DeepSeek-7B 0.29→0.38.
- Dynamic belief updating (Qwen2.5-7B): Avalon M1 0.58→0.71, M2 0.48→0.85, M3 0.41→0.80 — later missions gain the most (the mission index is not part of the target label; more evidence has accumulated). Werewolf: Day-1 0.42→0.53, Day-2 0.38→0.68, Day-3 0.34→0.75 — the largest gains occur at later game states. The policy integrates temporally accumulated evidence rather than merely emitting the right format.
- Role-specialized: the Assassin is the ONLY role with a clear, sustained improvement; response length DECREASES while reward increases — the policy learns minimal intermediate responses and reserves its effective decision for the final assassination. Merlin/Servant/Minion remain noisy (success depends on a broader behavioural mixture).
- Robustness: four independent seeds, Werewolf social-reading reward +20.2/+17.4/+35.7/+14.8 percentage points (first 20 vs last 20 updates); response length does not systematically increase — the gains are not verbosity.
- Vote steering (Minion): target hit rate DeepSeek-7B +28.7%, Qwen2.5-7B +11.8%, GLM-4-9B +5.8%, Qwen2.5-14B +3.2%, Qwen3-4B +2.1%. Wolf-protection: DeepSeek +36.5%, Qwen2.5-7B +12.2%, others ~3.3–3.4%. Werewolf-team win rate: DeepSeek +48.0%, Qwen2.5-7B +16.5%, Qwen2.5-14B +6.1%, GLM +5.8%, Qwen3-4B +4.1%. Response length is heterogeneous (Qwen2.5-7B much more verbose, DeepSeek moderate) — length alone does not explain steering success.
- Team approval (Avalon): target approval outcome improves for ALL models: Qwen2.5-7B +21.3%, Qwen2.5-14B +12.5%, Qwen3-4B +10.6%, DeepSeek-7B +16.3%, GLM-4-9B +18.1%; positive follow shifts (others vote in the target direction after the response); DeepSeek-7B improves despite shorter responses.
- Vote-steering robustness (Qwen2.5-7B, four runs): +16.5%/+7.0%/+26.3%/+10.9% — the direction replicates, the magnitude varies; all runs increase response length (one route for this model).
- Prompt-level ablations (Werewolf good-side win rate): SR+SI improves every model: Qwen2.5-7B 24→37% (+13pp), Qwen3-4B 21→34% (+13pp), Qwen2.5-14B 15→23% (+8pp), DeepSeek-7B 14→19% (+5pp), GLM-4-9B 19→22% (+3pp). SR alone improves all models; SI alone is variable — Qwen3-4B SI-only REDUCES performance substantially (12%) — influence planning is beneficial only when grounded in an accurate belief state.
- Full-game multi-agent RFT: six-player Werewolf win rate 0.25→~1.0, reward 0.15→0.57, social reading 0.05→0.70, social influence 0.10→0.60+. Nine-player: win 0.10→0.95–1.00, reward 0.10→0.60+, influence →0.70, reading above its initial value (less monotonic). Avalon: win ~0→0.65–0.75, reward 0.30→0.55, reading 0.85–0.90, influence →1.0. Win rate improves TOGETHER with the intermediate diagnostics even though it is evaluated separately.
- Cross-model generalization: the learned good-side policy wins 96% (vs Qwen3-4B) down to 72% (vs Kimi-K2.5); ≥90% against Qwen3.7-Max, Qwen-Plus, GLM-4-9B-Chat, and Qwen-Max. SR-F1 0.523–0.643, SI-Follow 0.583–0.725, Wolf-Exile 62.1%–90.8% — the behaviours transfer across opponent families rather than exploiting one opponent.
- Human evaluation (blinded, 178 ratings, 89 per condition): overall value 1.43→2.52 (+1.09); social reading 1.35→2.25 (+0.90); influence & coordination 1.54→2.70 (+1.16); strategic correctness 1.21→2.38 (+1.17); human-like gameplay 1.64→2.00 (+0.36, P = 0.067 — NOT significant). Sign-flip permutation tests P < 0.0001 for four dimensions (all but human-like). Matched pairs: RFT higher in 42/74 overall (tie 25, lower 7); influence 45/22/7; strategic 42/30/2; reading 32/34/8. Robust after removing the Qwen3.7-Plus-as-wolf overlap (59 pairs): overall +1.09, reading +0.86, influence +1.31, human-like +0.27, strategic +1.14. → RFT improves what agents infer and which actions they coordinate, but does NOT fully remove model-like linguistic behaviour — strategic competence and linguistic naturalness are partially dissociable.

## Actionable Insights
- Do NOT optimize win-rate directly for social agents — decompose the reward into social reading (belief inference scored against the hidden state) + social influence (message → downstream behaviour change), with terminal win as a held-out diagnostic. Example: a multi-agent customer-support system — reward correct user-intent inference and measured resolution-shift, not just ticket closure.
- Influence WITHOUT accurate beliefs is harmful: the SI-only prompt cut Qwen3-4B's win rate from 21% to 12%. Always ground persuasion in an explicit belief state first. Example: a negotiation agent — infer the counterpart's position before crafting a persuasive message; coordination amplifies wrong suspicions when beliefs are wrong.
- Accumulated evidence matters most: gains concentrate at later game states (Werewolf Day-3 F1 0.34→0.75 vs Day-1 0.42→0.53; Avalon M3 0.41→0.80). Design agents to aggregate accumulated evidence rather than act on static priors. Example: a meeting agent — revise its read of sentiment after each speaker, not only from the first message.

## Assumptions & Limitations
- Conditions: the games have explicit hidden states, discrete decisions, and well-defined team objectives — which make social computations measurable; rewards require operationalizing the intended social state and downstream effect; GRPO fits because rewards are sparse, noisy, and trajectory-dependent (no critic needed).
- Limitations acknowledged: real social environments have ambiguous identities, changing objectives, and contested definitions of success; the agents interact with fixed or slowly changing policies — simultaneous learning by multiple opposing agents could produce non-stationary dynamics, strategic escalation, or cyclic behaviour; the influence metrics establish that messages are FOLLOWED BY behavioural movement but do not identify the mechanism (evidence, rhetorical clarity, repetition, confidence, or model-specific exploitation); cross-LLM generalization ≠ generalization to humans (humans may interpret confidence, deception, uncertainty, and social commitment differently).
- Not stated: dual-use risk (influence mechanisms could steer choices, exploit social biases, or produce deceptive communication — discussed but no mitigation experiment); the human evaluation pool is small (178 ratings from invited evaluators, descriptive pooled estimates); the DeepSeek-7B Avalon curve is treated as a placeholder (reward shaping traded off evil-player recall against good-player accuracy); no LLM-as-judge comparison for the human evaluation; the response-length confound is not fully separable from the steering effect.
