# 2610.06069v1 Attention Tax, Handoff Tax: A Stylised Model of When Multi-Agent LLM Systems Help

## Problem
- The multi-agent LLM literature reaches sharply opposed conclusions: sceptics (Tran & Kiela — data processing inequality; Ao et al. — decision-theoretic dominance) prove a single agent with the same information and compute should dominate any delegated system; optimists (Tang et al.) prove multi-agent gains grow with task depth; Meyerson et al. solve a million-step task with zero errors via decomposition; Jwalapuram et al. put both outcomes in one table: self-consistency beats six automated multi-agent frameworks almost everywhere (often at under a tenth of the cost), yet an expert-designed decomposition raises GPT-5 from 57.0 to 96.5 on a context-heavy task. The contradiction comes from modelling different bottlenecks: each prior model fixes some of five quantities and lets the others move, and the quantity one side fixes is exactly the one the other side varies. No prior model holds all five quantities (attention tax — the cost of context load; handoff tax — the information loss when state is compressed into a summary; redundancy dependence — correlated failures across samples; verification; budget) to predict WHERE the winner flips.

## Contribution
- A stylised reliability model: a task is a chain of L steps; one step is one LLM call with failure probability ε(c, b) depending on context load c and thinking budget b, in log-loss units g(c,b) = −log(1−ε). Two structural primitives: decomposition (fresh context per agent, at the price of a handoff loss h) and redundancy (N parallel calls whose failures are dependent because they share a base model). Assumptions 1–4: product form (steps fail independently given (c,b)), monotone curve (ε nondecreasing in c, nonincreasing in b), context growth (single agent c_t = c0 + kt; the i-th step of a decomposed agent sees c0 + s + ki, s = summary length), handoff monotonicity (h ≥ 0, nonincreasing in s).
- Two crossover conditions: (a) decomposition becomes preferable once the attention cost avoided by resetting context exceeds the handoff cost — Theorem 1 (single-step agents: a unique L* exists iff a∞ > heff, where a_t is the attention tax at step t and heff = h + ∆o), Theorem 2 (arbitrary chunk sizes: some chunk size m beats the single agent for all large L iff A∞ > h, the total avoidable tax), Theorem 3 (linear-regime closed form: L* = max{2, ⌊2(h+λs)/(λk)⌋+1}), Theorem 4 (chunk-size square-root law: m* = √(2h/(λ(k−2s/L)))), Proposition 1 (knee-shaped curves), Proposition 2 (summary-length optimum: −h′(s*) = λ); (b) parallel sampling at equal budget is eventually preferable when its shared-failure floor lies below the thinking floor of one agent thinking longer — Theorem 5 (majority vote under exchangeable failures converges to the shared-failure mass F = µ(p>1/2) + ½µ(p=1/2)), Theorem 6 (parallel wins for all large N iff F < ε∞(c); a sufficient condition using only (ε_b, ρ): F_max = ρε(1−ε)/(ρε(1−ε)+(½−ε)²) < ε∞(c)), Corollary 3 (an upper bound on F), Corollary 4 (the million-step regime: at L = 10⁶ both the per-step vote failure and the handoff tax must be ~10⁻⁶, which requires passing the complete explicit state, not a summary).
- Prior results recovered as restrictions: Tran & Kiela = flat curve (Corollary 1 — the sceptics' conclusion is the flat corner: the single agent weakly dominates every decomposition at every depth); Ao et al. = flat curve with a capacity-free centre (analogical); Tang et al. = ρ=0 redundancy with free compute (structural); Meyerson et al. = m=1 with per-step voting and full state passed (Corollary 4, empirical); Xu et al. = task noise as h and model noise as the curve (analogical).
- Two real measurements: (a) a padded-context pilot on gpt-oss-20b (1,000 calls, five loads); (b) a ledger-reconciliation experiment (40,600 calls, 309M prompt tokens, ~$10, 27 minutes) that measures the in-task curve and the handoff tax from single-agent and handoff runs alone, and predicts the crossover at depth 10 and the decomposed system's success to within 9 percentage points at depths 20, 50, and 100.

## Method
- The handoff tax (Definition 1) is operational: h = G_M − G_H + Σ[g(c0+|H|+ki, b) − g(c0+s+ki, b)] — run the same chunk from the summary M and from the full workspace H, adding the curve term back; measurable, not merely counterfactual.
- Pilot: gpt-oss-20b (Ollama, 20.9B parameters, MXFP4, native context 131,072), Ollama 0.30.8, one RTX 3090, num_ctx 71,680, flash attention, 8-bit KV cache, reasoning level low, temperature 0.7, output budget 1,024 tokens. Task: chained arithmetic word problems (six operations, exact integer answer), 100 problems × 2 samples, context padding at nominal 0/2k/8k/32k/64k tokens (measured 107/2,117/8,023/31,641/63,129 tokens).
- Ledger experiment: a ledger of L transactions with an exact integer running balance starting at 50,000; six kinds (deposit, withdrawal, percentage fee rounded down, hold, release returning a hold 3–12 steps later named only by id, reversal undoing a deposit/withdrawal 3–12 steps back named only by id — the last two are the long-range dependencies); each block padded to ~400 tokens (measured 398 tokens/step); the same 100 ledgers at each depth for paired comparison. Single agent: one growing conversation, c0 = 197 tokens. Decomposed: m = 5 consecutive transactions per fresh agent; a handoff note of ~300 tokens (225 words) carrying the balance, every open hold, and the id and amount of the last 12 transactions. The handoff tax was measured on a separate pool of 100 ledgers with a paired design (12 transactions continued from history, the next 5 started from a note, at note allowances of 100/300/900 tokens → produced notes of 137/183/255 tokens). gpt-oss-20b on a hosted OpenAI-compatible endpoint, reasoning low, temperature 0.7, a 512-token allowance per call (b) for both systems; handoff notes are separate calls with a 600-token allowance (∆o = 0). Grading: per-step increment failure (stationary = excluding fee steps) and exact final balance; exact McNemar paired tests.

## Key Findings
- Pilot — two curves from the same 1,000 calls: arithmetic failures FLAT: 5.5/5.0/5.5/3.5/6.0% (paired 64k-vs-rest +1.1pp, SE 1.9, t99 = 0.59; slope CI [−6.1,+6.6]×10⁻⁷ per token — flat within four points over 63k tokens). Format failures (missing ANSWER: line): 0/0.5/5.0/6.5/25.5% (+22.5pp, SE 3.4, t99 = 6.7; in 47 of 51 failures the number was still right — the model stopped stating it in the required form, and a strict parser downstream fails on all 51). Strict: 5.5/5.5/10.0/10.0/29.5%. Weighted hinge fit: knee near 27k tokens, post-knee slope 7.8×10⁻⁶ per token [3.4, 11.1]×10⁻⁶. → the curve is a property of the model, the task, and the failure definition together.
- Ledger in-task curve (17,000 single-agent steps): failure rises from 2.7% (<2k tokens) to 71% (>36k) — far steeper than the pilot because every step depends on the accumulated trajectory. Stationary kinds: 2.1/4.9/6.6/12.9/22.7/54.8/68.2% across load bins. Reasoning grew with load (20 tokens/step below 4k → 153 above 30k); 5.1% of single-agent calls hit the 512-token budget vs 0.13% of decomposed calls. Fee steps measure the state carried in, not the step: at constant load fee failure rises 7%→72% after the 50th transaction; conditioned on a correct preceding balance it is flat (5/91 early, 3/55 late; 283/340 late fees following a wrong balance fail).
- Handoff tax: continuing from history (mean load 6.2k), post-boundary stationary steps failed 5.2%; starting from a note (mean load 1.6k): 3.2/2.0/1.8% for the three note lengths. The raw comparison favours the note (history sits higher on the curve); Definition 1 adds the curve term back (0.172 nats per chunk) → increment tax h_inc = 0.077/0.026/0.015 nats for notes of 137/183/255 tokens. Balance preservation: 100/99/100 of 100 calibration ledgers; h_bal = −log(0.99) = 0.010; total taxes 0.077/0.036/0.015 nats, nonincreasing in note length. Bootstrap 95% CIs: increment tax [−0.11, 0.16], total [−0.10, 0.18] — negative projections are set to zero per Assumption 4.
- Prediction and result: crossover L* = 10 (bootstrap quantiles 6 and 27). Predicted vs observed (single/decomposed): L=20: predicted stationary 0.46/0.61, observed 0.50/0.69, final 0.49/0.64, McNemar p = 0.014/0.049; L=50: predicted 0.01/0.27, observed stationary 0.06/0.24, final 0.02/0.18, p = 0.001/0.003; L=100: predicted 0.00/0.07, observed stationary 0.00/0.11, final 0.00/0.10, p <0.001/0.002. Decomposition won at every tested depth on both gradings; paired improvements 0.19/0.18/0.11 with bootstrap CIs [0.05,0.33]/[0.08,0.28]/[0.05,0.18]. Predictions miss observations by at most 9 percentage points (single agent: +4/+5/0 points above the prediction — the direction the product form implies when failures cluster on hard ledgers; decomposed: +8.4/−3.1/+3.9, no consistent sign). Sensitivity with h_joint = 0.067: decomposed predictions 0.55/0.20/0.04, still ahead at every depth. No-truncation subset (98/62/12 pairs): final-balance 0.49 vs 0.65, 0.02 vs 0.16, 0 vs 0.08.
- Token usage: the single agent generated 806/2,873/10,046 tokens per ledger at depths 20/50/100 vs the decomposed system's 1,611/4,797/10,195 (including notes; 770/2,001/4,078 for step calls alone); at depth 100 the two systems generated the same number of tokens; on the input side the single agent processed 2.0M prompt tokens per ledger at depth 100 vs 0.2M for the decomposed system (it re-reads its growing context at every step).
- Chunk size: with the primary h = 0.036 nats and the low-load slope ~8×10⁻⁶ per token, Theorem 4's approximation gives √(2h/(λk)) ≈ 4.8 steps — close to the m = 5 chosen before measuring anything.
- Regime placement: the flat region (small k, short contexts) is multi-hop QA and static reasoning benchmarks (Jwalapuram: self-consistency ahead of six automated frameworks on GPQA-Diamond/HLE-Maths, at under a tenth of the cost; redundancy still helps via Theorem 5). The rising region (large k or large c0, a decomposable task) is the SMFR task: expert decomposition takes GPT-5 from 57.0 to 96.5 ($554.82 vs $478.40) and GPT-OSS-120B from 26.1 to 36.1; the automated frameworks gain at most 6.6 points at 2–5× the cost (they pay orchestration overhead without buying much attention-tax reduction). Competency floor: GPT-4o moves 22.1→22.6 under the expert decomposition — nothing; if ε at minimal context is already large, decomposition removes the attention tax but the fresh agents' product is still near zero.
- Redundancy threshold (Theorem 6, worked example): ε(b) = 0.08 + 0.22e^(−b) at b = 1 gives a threshold correlation ρ* = 0.497; ρ ∈ {0, 0.2} favour parallel sampling and ρ ∈ {0.5, 0.8} favour sequential thinking at every N ∈ {3, 5, 11, 51, 201}. Ledger illustration: ε = 5.5%, ρ = 0.12 → common-shock floor 0.7%, bound 3.1% — parallel sampling beats longer thinking for large N whenever the thinking floor is above 3.1%.

## Actionable Insights
- Measure before you orchestrate: run your single agent at increasing context loads on YOUR task, fit the failure-vs-load curve; measure the handoff tax with paired continue-vs-handoff runs; compute the crossover L* = max{2, ⌊2(h+λs)/(λk)⌋+1} (linear regime). If your tasks sit below L*, use one agent — multi-agent orchestration is a pure loss. Example: document QA with small per-step context growth → single agent; tool-heavy chains with k≈400+ tokens/step → decompose past depth ~10.
- Chunk size follows the square-root law: each subagent should own ≈ √(2h/(λk)) steps (with overhead added to the handoff tax). Cheap handoffs and steep curves → one step per agent; expensive handoffs or flat curves → one agent for everything. Example: with h = 0.036 nats, slope 8×10⁻⁶ per token, k = 400 → ~4.8 steps per agent.
- The failure definition is part of the curve: the same 1,000 calls gave a flat curve (arithmetic) and a steep one (format compliance) — decay of compliance with an instruction sitting far from its point of use, not reasoning collapse. Repeat the format instruction next to the question to flatten the strict curve; a paper showing single-agent wins on short contexts and multi-agent wins on long ones may be measuring instruction survival, not capability.
- Parallel vs sequential at equal budget: majority vote over N samples wins for large N iff the shared-failure mass F is below the thinking floor ε∞(c); a sufficient check uses only (ε_b, ρ): F_max < ε∞. Heterogeneous ensembles flip more tasks to parallel; homogeneous self-consistency saturates. Example: before trusting an agent ensemble's voting gains, measure the pairwise failure correlation ρ between samples.

## Assumptions & Limitations
- Conditions: product form (steps fail independently given (c,b)) — ignores within-trajectory dependence and overstates the single agent's loss when failures cluster on hard instances (the direction seen on the ledger); monotone curve; the handoff tax is one scalar per boundary (related to Ao et al.'s information-theoretic loss only by analogy); budget fixed per step; the redundancy floor depends on the whole mixing law — a measured (ε, ρ) bounds but does not pin the floor; the vote is modelled on the correctness indicator (pessimistic for open-ended answers with multiple wrong values).
- Limitations acknowledged: the measurements cover one model (gpt-oss-20b) on two tasks; the quantities needed next are the boundary verifier recall, the thinking floor, and curves for further models; the pilot measures padded session context, not the distribution a real trajectory accumulates; the two sections' curves are not quantitatively comparable (different serving stacks); Proposition 3 is conditional on an untested hypothesis (a fresh-context verifier is less dependent than a self-check — needs the same verifier prompt in both positions); runs at depths on both sides of the crossover are the next measurement (the crossover at depth 10 sits below the tested range).
- Not stated: the linear-regime closed forms are only local fits (the curve shape is guessed over the range of loads a trajectory visits); the knee location is loosely determined (the robust feature is only the rise from ~10% to ~30% between 32k and 63k); temperature 0.7 with a fixed budget may not represent production serving; the handoff-note content (balance + holds + 12 transactions) is a design choice — the tax would change with the note schema; the verifier recall is not actually measured (Proposition 3 remains conditional).
