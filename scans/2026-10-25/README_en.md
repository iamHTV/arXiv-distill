## 5 papers distilled today

### [1] Extending FunctionGemma: Local Android control with a small function-calling model (arXiv 2609.25373v2)
- What it does: Extends the FunctionGemma model (270 million parameters / parameters are the memory variables inside an AI model, more means heavier) to control an Android device locally (runs on the phone, not sent to the cloud) — turning on the flashlight, sending SMS, opening apps, taking photos — instead of only calling web APIs (addresses an app calls to fetch data).
- Key takeaway: A small model achieves 76.5% accuracy across 15 device action categories using about 9,500 synthetic samples (data generated by machine), without calling a large cloud model per request. Accuracy rose from 29.3% (base model) to 76.5% (+47.2 points).
- Apply to you: Build a phone assistant that performs fixed actions (open Zalo app, compose a message, save a contact, schedule a callback) without sending customer messages to a cloud server — good for customer data security. Since 76.5% accuracy means about 1 in 4 commands is wrong, require confirmation before money-related actions (discount codes, order confirmation) and measure accuracy per action category rather than one overall score.

### [2] SPRA: Structured paper representation for AI agents (arXiv 2610.21424v2)
- What it does: Turns research papers into structured objects (problem, contribution, component, experiment setup, measure, finding) with labeled relations between them, so AI agents can look up, browse, and reuse paper content without reading the full text.
- Key takeaway: Reduces tokens (units of text processed by AI) by 28.5% vs. passage retrieval and 95.3% vs. full text; achieves a semantic quality score of 2.342/5, beating passage retrieval at 1.961/5. The dataset contains over 1 million AI papers, 19.95 million research units, and 24.07 million relations.
- Apply to you: Instead of storing full document text, extract 6 roles (problem, contribution, component, setup, measure, finding) and the relations between them so your AI assistant can look up quickly and verify sources — lowering text-processing cost and answering business or legal questions more accurately.

### [3] Agentic RAG for enterprise retail workflows over heterogeneous data sources (arXiv 2610.25096v1)
- What it does: Builds RetailRAG, an agentic RAG system (autonomously decides how to search and combine information from multiple sources) tailored to retail, routing queries across multiple data sources (structured tables + unstructured text) instead of dumping everything into one single database.
- Key takeaway: RetailRAG achieves an LLM-judge (score graded by an AI model, scale 0-1) of 0.429 on average, beating the strongest baseline HetaRAG (0.336), and uses over 3x fewer API calls (each AI model call costs money) (7.45 vs 25.21) on the RetailBridge benchmark (a standard test set) with 1,680 multi-source queries. Connecting evidence across sources is the dominant structural trap (the structural flaw causing most failures) for retail agents.
- Apply to you: Instead of dumping all product info, customer chat history, and price lists into one AI prompt (prompt: instruction sent to the AI), organize data into separate "buckets" (product category, customer reviews, policies) and tell the AI which bucket to search for each type of question — more accurate answers and lower API call cost.

### [4] Business Arena: Evaluating LLM agents in a realistic marketplace (arXiv 2608.08621)
- What it does: Builds a simulated cross-border shop environment to evaluate 15 LLM models running a business over time (stocking, pricing, customer service, regulatory compliance).
- Key takeaway: Final net worth ranges from $20,856 to $188,488 (a 9.0x gap); 51% of runs lost money; only 4 models preserved initial capital; the best expert strategy reached $436,195 (over 2x the best model). Multi-dimensional evaluation (cash flow, fraud avoidance, customer service, compliance) matters more than one aggregate score.
- Apply to you: When hiring or evaluating an AI agent for your business, don't just look at one aggregate number — measure capital preservation, pricing, customer service, and regulatory compliance separately to know where the agent is strong or weak.

### [5] E-Commerce Bench: Evaluating autonomous selling agents over one year (arXiv 2608.30730v1)
- What it does: Builds the first open-source benchmark to evaluate autonomous store agents over one year (365 days), combining partner negotiation, dynamic events, and multi-store management.
- Key takeaway: A 1,264x gap between best and worst models; one model reached a final asset of 1,431,425 Yuan (14.3x capital) but ranked 16/18 on fraud avoidance; no model leads on every dimension, so multi-dimensional diagnosis is needed instead of one single score.
- Apply to you: When evaluating an automated selling assistant, set up a fixed test environment (identical for every run) and measure multiple dimensions (profit, avoiding fraudulent suppliers, cash flow, learning from experience) instead of just one number, to avoid being misled by luck or an aggregate score that hides weaknesses.
