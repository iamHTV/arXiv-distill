## Today's 11 papers

### [1] But How Would AI Agents Run a Town's Economy? (arXiv 2609.11108v1)
- **What it does:** Simulates a town of 100 AI agents (autonomous programs) trading with each other (selling, paying wages, transferring money), then applies a demand shock (demand suddenly increases 12×) to see how money circulates over 311 pulses (rounds).
- **What we learned:** Demand 12× → revenue 4.62×, decomposed into 1.50× extensive margin (number of firms trading: 304→458 of 762 firms) × 3.07× intensive margin (value per transaction). Wages nearly unchanged (1.03×, p=0.42); a 5,000-unit transfer: 96.7% still circulating after 311 pulses; MPC (marginal propensity to consume — share of extra money spent) 3.3–4.0%. Model choice affects results 3–10×, more than memory; social tools (invite_to_talk, leave_conversation) fail 94–100%, economic tools succeed 96%.
- **Apply it:** When running a "demand shock" flash sale, revenue grows mostly from existing customers buying more (intensive margin 3.07×), not new customers (1.50×) — stock up for regular customers and don't expect new-customer volume to scale with the campaign.

### [2] Social Gym and SPARTAN: Measuring LLM Social Reasoning via Multi-Agent Game Tournaments (arXiv 2608.09128v1)
- **What it does:** An arena of 21 social games (normal-form, economic, bluffing, hidden-role deduction like Werewolves, social strategy) with rule-decided outcomes — no AI judging AI — then ranks 7 models with an Elo (rating) system (mean anchor 1000, 560+ episodes/model).
- **What we learned:** GPT-5-mini leads at 1110 Elo, 63.5% win rate; Qwen3-32B is first on Chicken (1328 Elo) but last on Werewolves (817) — no model is strong at every game. SPARTAN (Self-Play and Reflect-Transfer; training-free): writes a game-agnostic playbook (tactical playbook) from self-play, prepends it to the system prompt → the structurally weaker side wins 24%→43% (peak at reflection round 3), with most of the gain at round 1 (extra rounds only redistribute); cross-game transfer +7pp median; distillation to weaker models +13–24pp; but Qwen3-32B learns almost nothing (capacity-dependent).
- **Apply it:** Don't trust a single test for your customer-service AI — the same model can be great at negotiation but bad at crisis handling. Build a mini "Social Gym": 5–10 scenarios with clear answers (offer the discount? keep brand tone?), score with a true/false checklist, don't ask another AI to grade.

### [3] Continuous Improvement and Parallel Autonomous Exploration (arXiv 2608.04341v1)
- **What it does:** AI agents autonomously compete to improve product-to-catalog matching on an e-commerce marketplace (33,000 SKUs — stock-keeping units), with a withheld-test leaderboard as the reward signal, running 1 or 5 agents in parallel, no human in the loop.
- **What we learned:** Qualified coverage (Precision@1 ≥95% per category) 33.3% (BGE-M3 baseline) → 47.8–57.4% (1 agent) / 62.8–69.4% (5 agents). A single agent falls into a structural trap: it stays on the seeded embedding (vector representation) direction and never tries the basic lexical (keyword) method; 5 agents independently discover non-embedding methods (TF-IDF, string matching) within minutes; the winning method arrives late (65.6%→69.4%).
- **Apply it:** When letting AI self-optimize (e.g., rewriting product descriptions), don't run a single agent — it locks into one direction. Run 3–5 agents with different approaches in parallel and take the best; and always test the "boring" method (keywords, templates) before concluding the AI way is best.

### [4] When Truth Is Distributed: Misinformation Derails Collective Fact Recovery (arXiv 2608.03421v3)
- **What it does:** The ForesightSafety-TIDE framework simulates 5 AI agents recovering facts from distributed evidence (each sees only part), where one key role knows the ground truth but lies (deceptive) in 3 forms (fabricated event, coverage, source), 120 environments × 3 models (GPT-5.5, DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Grok-4.5).
- **What we learned:** Majority votes correctly 72.50% (all honest) → 14.17% (one deceiver) — a 58.33pp drop. False testimony is adopted 70.7% vs true testimony 25.4%; false claims propagate deeper (2.66 vs 1.33 levels); misinformation persists after the deceiver exits (False-Exit reduces 46.67pp vs control). Adding observers: cuts false consensus 90% but doesn't increase true consensus — converts to "unknown" instead of correcting errors.
- **Apply it:** When multiple AIs "meet" to analyze data (e.g., 5 agents summarizing customer feedback), one agent with corrupted data can drag the whole group down — always trace the provenance (origin) of each piece of evidence, distinguish "heard directly" vs "heard secondhand", and allow an "UNKNOWN" verdict instead of forcing consensus.

### [5] CURATE: Composing, Cataloging, and Deploying Reproducible Workflows (arXiv 2608.04270v2)
- **What it does:** A 4-agent system (WCA/MCA/MGA/WDA) managing the full workflow lifecycle: sketch → reify → test → deploy, with 5 gates for human-in-the-loop review, and a module catalog (reusable module library, BM25 search).
- **What we learned:** 6 experiments: recreates 3 standard SeBS-Flow workflows (E1 Random Forest accuracy 0.850±0.002 vs original 0.851±0.006; E3 video analysis 0 runtime errors vs baseline 0/3 successes); reuses a MapReduce subgraph from the catalog to build a new workflow (E4, 4 modules reused verbatim); automates and scales a wastewater-treatment simulation (PyADM1) with 20 concurrent inputs (E6) — the WDA (Workflow Deployment Agent) catches a hard-coded q_ad parameter and suggests a fix; token usage nearly halves from E5 to E6 thanks to catalog reuse.
- **Apply it:** When automating with AI (e.g., a content pipeline: fetch RSS → summarize → post to blog), don't let the AI rewrite from scratch each time — store verified steps in a catalog, compose new workflows from reuse + generate only new modules; and always keep one "human review" gate before deploying to production.

### [6] MLExpert: A Generalist Agentic Model for Autonomous ML Engineering (arXiv 2610.22344v1)
- **What it does:** Trains a 35B model (3B active — MoE: mixture-of-experts) to run the full MLE (machine learning engineering) loop: propose ideas → write code → run experiments → submit results, on 22 Kaggle tasks (MLE-Bench Lite).
- **What we learned:** Medal rate 37.9% (base Qwen3.6-35B-A3B) → 72.7% (MLExpert), on par with GLM-5.3/Kimi-K3, below GPT-5.6 Sol (77.3%). Pipeline: general SFT (supervised fine-tuning) → MLE-domain SFT (prefix trimming — keep the prefix up to the first best submission) → WIRL (Windowed Interaction RL: split the trajectory into windows between interactions, reward −1.0 execution error / −0.6 no scorable submission / −0.4+T_m below medal / 1.0+T_m bronze-silver / 2.0+T_m gold, T_m = trend −0.2/0/+0.2). GRPO 56.1% and PPO 60.6% both fall below the MLE-SFT baseline of 62.1% — the windowed design matters. Generalization: NatureBench Lite Surpass-SOTA 0%→50%; optimized scaffold AIME 23.3%→43.7% held-out accuracy; algorithm-component optimization on MLS-Bench-Lite mean reward 0.322→0.425.
- **Apply it:** When using an AI agent for long tasks (e.g., multi-step sales-data analysis), don't reward once at the end — split into windows between steps, reward each window (did it submit? did it improve?) and cut the tail after the first failure (keep the prefix up to the first best submission).

### [7] Bayes-Sufficient Compression Is Not Enough: How Does Communication Help Multi-Agent Systems? (arXiv 2610.03769v1)
- **What it does:** A theoretical framework + 6 benchmarks (BFCL, ToolSandbox, ContextBench, RepoBench, SWE-bench Lite, τ-bench) measuring when a short message (compression) helps the executor, when raw context is better, and when a stronger sender helps.
- **What we learned:** Communication-Tax Identity: utility U = R(0)−R(1) = A*−τ (gain = max accuracy − protocol tax). ContextBench: 0.633→0.775 (128-token message); but ToolSandbox 0.889→0.653, BFCL 0.980→0.750 — compression hurts. Sender-upgrade crossover threshold 0.6289 (89.5% of 38 anchors follow a single crossing). Fixed-message replay analysis: GLM-5 recovers the artifact 74.1% but converts to correct action only 54.5%; MiniMax-M2.5: 61.3% vs 5.3% — closure (action packaging) errors dominate, not content-understanding errors. Protocol selector: accuracy 0.7367 at 1,735 tokens/step — +8.67pp over always-skip (0.6500) and −46.4% tokens vs always-send (3,235 tokens/step).
- **Apply it:** When a child agent needs context from a parent agent, don't default to "summarize it shorter" — for easy tasks (raw context already >95% accuracy), messaging hurts (ToolSandbox −23.6pp). Use the rule: high local accuracy → skip; medium → compress to 128 tokens; low → send raw context; and always check "did the executor turn understanding into correct action" (closure), not just "did it understand".

### [8] Everyone Conforms, No One Believes: Pluralistic Ignorance in LLM Agent Populations (arXiv 2608.02758v1)
- **What it does:** A benchmark of 100 scenarios × 10 domains (campus, climate, workplace, politics...) × 8 models, simulating 20 agents (16 dissenters — privately opposed, 4 supporters) discussing for 2 rounds, then 1 norm entrepreneur speaking out + 2 more rounds.
- **What we learned:** Pluralistic ignorance (the majority privately rejects the norm but publicly conforms, each believing they alone dissent) appears in every model: conformity rate 64.2% (GPT-4o-mini) → 93.7% (Kimi-K2.7); domain-sensitive (social relationships 96%, workplace 90% vs politics 78%) but capability-INDEPENDENT (Spearman ρ=0.07, p=0.87); authority level doesn't matter (weak peer signal ≈ top-down mandate). Cascades after intervention: 7/8 models <26%, Kimi-K2.7 = 0%, GPT-4o outlier 48%. Ablation: removing the false-consensus statement only −3.2pp on average; removing the "fit in" goal −14pp; removing both still leaves 52–92% — emergent, not instruction-driven. Second-order belief errors: agents underestimate the true 80% opposition by 14–38pp.
- **Apply it:** When surveying customers with AI, "the majority agrees" may be an illusion — everyone thinks they're the only one who dislikes it. Always ask privately (private belief) before asking publicly; and one voice speaking out can break the illusion (GPT-4o: 87%→58%), so actively seek dissenting voices in customer group chats.

### [9] ARGUS: Multi-Agent Forensic Reasoning for Generalizable Deepfake Video Detection (arXiv 2608.06865v1)
- **What it does:** 4 observation agents (texture, lighting, motion, physics) each watch a video from one angle and write observations WITHOUT a verdict, then 1 judge (arbitrator) synthesizes the observations + video to rule real/fake. Dataset FaceVid-Forensics-100K: 100,000 videos, 33 synthesis methods (including Seedance 2.0, Sora 2, Kling).
- **What we learned:** Out-of-distribution (OOD — unseen generators): accuracy 69.87%, recall 81.82%, F1 53.28% — best, +5.83pp F1 over the strongest single-model baseline (Gemini-2.5-Pro 47.45%). Ablation: texture cues are strongest (recall 97.40% with texture only); removing the video from the judge drops F1 53.28→51.01; GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) adds +2.24pp F1 over SFT alone. Multi-turn/CoT (chain-of-thought) on a single MLLM (multimodal LLM) hurts (Qwen2.5-VL-7B F1 13.24→9.50–11.92) — the improvement comes from independent evidence collection + reconciliation, not longer prompts.
- **Apply it:** When receiving a video/image from a "customer" or "partner" via Zalo/email, don't trust your eyes — have the AI examine 4 angles (skin/lighting/finger motion/finger shape) and require written evidence before a verdict. Example: fingers "plastic-smooth, missing nails, color changing across frames" = sign of AI synthesis.

### [10] LLM4AIGQ: LLM-based AI Guidance Query Generation for Multi-Interest Mining (arXiv 2609.03674v1)
- **What it does:** A 3-stage SFT→RL→DPO framework for an LLM to generate shopping guidance queries per user multi-interest on Taobao/Tmall — segment the user into ≤3 interest clusters, generate 3 queries per cluster, deploy nearline (pre-generate) + online (retrieve).
- **What we learned:** S ratio (share of queries at rating S — specific customer group/scenario + parameter comparison) 0.6405 (Qwen3-30B-A3B backbone) → 0.8616 (ONESFT+RL+DPO, +22.1pp); B ratio (queries unmappable to products/unsafe) 0.1043→0.0274. Recall@10 0.1771→0.2377 (+34.2%) thanks to DPO (Direct Preference Optimization); preference-pair filtering (remove the top 30% most similar pairs) +8.9%. Win rate vs teacher DeepSeek-V4-Pro: 34.63%→50.25% (SFT) →57.53% (RL) →54.95% (DPO). Online A/B test on Tmall: +4.46% uCTR (user click-through rate) at 10% traffic, +2.53% at 40% traffic over 6 days; deployed to hundreds of millions of users. Short-title compression: −26.8% input tokens, −10.7% RT (response time).
- **Apply it:** When recommending products to a customer with mixed taste (e.g., just bought a camera and also likes coffee), don't merge into one list — split into 2 interest clusters, write 3 suggestions per cluster, each ≤15 characters, specific ("light travel camera under $500", not "cool gadgets").

## WHAT YOU CAN APPLY

1. **Running a single agent = hitting a wall.** Paper [3]: a lone agent never tries the basic method; 5 parallel agents find the winning method within minutes. → When letting AI optimize anything (product descriptions, ad headlines), run 3–5 agents with different approaches and take the best.

2. **Reward per window, not at the finish line.** Paper [6]: WIRL (rewarding each window between interactions) reaches 72.7% medal rate; classic GRPO/PPO both fall below baseline. → When an AI agent does a multi-step task (data analysis, report writing), score after each step (did it submit? did it improve?) and discard the tail after the first mistake.

3. **Don't let AI grade AI — use rules with answers.** Paper [2]: 21 games with rule-decided outcomes → verifiable; paper [9]: 4 evidence angles + judge. → Build your own true/false checklist for content (discount offered? tone correct? link alive?), score by rules, don't ask another model to grade.

4. **Falsehood spreads deeper than truth — always trace the source.** Paper [4]: false testimony adopted 70.7% vs true 25.4%, propagates 2.66 vs 1.33 levels, persists after the deceiver leaves. → When multiple AIs "meet" to analyze data, tag the source (heard directly vs secondhand) on every piece of evidence, and allow an "UNKNOWN" verdict instead of forcing consensus.

5. **"The majority agrees" may be an illusion.** Paper [8]: pluralistic ignorance in every model (64–94%), emergent even without instructions; one dissenting voice breaks the illusion (87%→58%). → Customer surveys: ask privately first, publicly second; actively seek dissenting voices in groups/reviews before concluding "customers love the new product".
